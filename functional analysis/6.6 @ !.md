Let $X$ and $Y$ be compact metric spaces. Use the Stone-Weierstrass Theorem to show that any function $f \in C(X$ x $Y)$ can be uniformly approximated by functions of the form $F(x,y) = \sum_{i=1}^n f_i(x)g_i(y)$, where $f_i \in C(X)$ and $g_i \in C(Y), i=1,...,n$.

**Step 1: Define the candidate algebra**
Let $A = \{ \sum_{i=1}^n f_i(x)g_i(y) : f_i \in C(X), g_i \in C(Y), n \in \mathbb{N} \}$.
Then:
- $A \subset C(X$ x $Y)$, 
- $A$ is closed under addition and scalar multiplication,
- $A$ is closed under multiplication since $(f(x)g(y))(f'(x) g'(y)) = (ff')(x) (gg')(y)$.
Thus, $A$ is a subalgebra of $C(X$x$Y)$.

**Step 2: Constants are included**
The constant function $1$ belongs to $A$ since $1(x,y) = 1 \cdot 1$, where $1 \in C(X)$ and $1 \in C(Y)$.

**Step 3: Separate of points**
We show that $A$ separates points in $X$x$Y$.
Take two distinct points $(x_1, y_1) \ne (x_2, y_2)$.  Then either: $x_1 \ne x_2$ or $y_1 \ne y_2$.
Case 1: $x_1 \ne x_2$
Since $X$ is compact metric (hence normal), there exists $f \in C(X)$ s.t. $f(x_1) \ne f(x_2)$. Let $g(y) = 1$, which is clearly in $C(Y)$. Then $F(x,y) = f(x)g(y) = f(x)$, so $F \in A$. Now evaluate: $F(x_1, y_1) = f(x_1)$ and $F(x_2, y_2) = f(x_2)$. Since $f(x_1) \ne f(x_2)$, we get $F(x_1, y_1) \ne F(x_2,y_2)$. So $A$ separates these two points.
Case 2: $y_1 \ne y_2$
Similarly, there exists $g \in C(Y)$ with $g(y_1) \ne g(y_2)$, and taking $f(x) = 1$, similarly $A$ separates these two points.
Thus, $A$ separates points of $X$x$Y$.

**Step 4: Apply Stone-Weirstrass**
By the Stone-Weierstrass Theorem, since:
- $X$x$Y$ is compact,
- $A$ is a subalgebra of $C(XxY)$,
- $A$ contains constants,
- $A$ separate points,
it follows that $A$ is dense in $C(X$x$Y)$ with respect to the uniform norm.

**Step 5: Conclusion**
Therefore, for every $f \in C(X$x$Y)$ and every $\epsilon > 0$, there exists a function $F(x,y) = \sum_{i=1}^n f_i(x)g_i(y)$ s.t $sup_{(x,y)\in X \times Y} |f(x,y) - F(x,y)| < \epsilon$.

**Final Statement**
Every continuous function on $X \times Y$ can be uniformly approximated by finite sums of separable functions $f_i(x)g_i(y)$. 

Notes
- It says: "complicated functions can be built from simple pieces" So instead of handling an arbitrary 2-variable function, you can approximate it using building blocks that: depend only on $x$ , or depend only on $y$. 
- It's the foundation of "tensor product" thinking. What you proved is essentially: $C(X \times Y) \approx C(X) \otimes C(Y)$ ("approximate" = dense in the uniform norm). This idea shows up everywhere: functional analysis, quantum mechanics (separable states vs entangled ones), PDEs (separation of variables). So this problem is an encounter with tensor product structure in function spaces.
- "Approximate" means uniformly $\sup_{(x,y) \in X \times Y} | F(x,y) - F_{approx}(x,y) | < \epsilon$.

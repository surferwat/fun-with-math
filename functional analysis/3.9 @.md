Show that if $U$ is a linear subspace of a normed space $X$ then $\overline{U}$ is a closed linear subspace of $X$.

WTS: $\overline{U}$ is closed.
$\overline{U}$ is the intersection of the closed subsets of $X$ that contain $U$.
Since the intersection of closed sets are closed, $\overline{U}$ is closed.
WTS: $\overline{U}$ is closed on under addition and scalar multiplication.
Let $x,y \in \overline{U}$.
Let $x_n, y_n \in U$ s.t. $x_n \to x$ (i.e., $\Vert x_n - x \Vert = 0$) and $y_n \to y$ (i.e., $\Vert y_n - y \Vert = 0$).
Addition:
$\Vert x_n - x \Vert + \Vert y_n - y \Vert=0$.
Using Minkowski's Inequality, $\Vert x_n - x \Vert + \Vert y_n - y \Vert \ge \Vert (x_n - x) + (y_n - y) \Vert = \Vert (x_n+y_n) - (x + y) \Vert$.
So, $\Vert (x_n+y_n) - (x + y) \Vert = 0$.
Thus, $(x_n+y_n) \to (x + y)$.
Since $(x+y)$ is a limit point of $(x_n+y_n) \in U$, $(x+y) \in \overline{U}$.
Scalar multiplication:
$\Vert x_n - x \Vert=0$.
 Let $\alpha \in \mathbb{K}$.
 $\alpha \Vert x_n - x \Vert=\alpha0$.
 $\Vert \alpha (x_n - x) \Vert=0$.
 $\Vert \alpha x_n - \alpha x \Vert=0$.
 Thus, $\alpha x_n \to \alpha x$.
 Since $\alpha x$ is a limit point of $\alpha x_n \in U$, $\alpha x \in \overline{U}$.
Thus, $\overline{U}$ is linear.

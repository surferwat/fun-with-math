Suppose that $Y$ is a proper finite-dimensional subspace of a normed space $X$. Show that for any $y \in Y$ and $r > 0$ there exists $x \in X$ such that $\Vert x - y \Vert = dist(x,Y) = r$ (Megginson, 1998).

**Step 1: Use finite dimensionality of $Y$**
Because $Y$ is finite-dimensional, it is closed in $X$. Hence for any $x \in X$, the distance $dist(x,Y) = \inf_{z \in Y} \Vert x - z \Vert$ is attained: there exists $y_0 \in Y$ s.t. $\Vert x - y_0 \Vert = dist(x, Y)$. 

**Step 2: Start with a vector outside $Y$**
Choose $x_0 \in X$ \ $Y$.
Let $y_0 \in Y$ be a best approximation to $x_0$, so $\Vert x_0 - y_0 \Vert = dist(x_0, Y) > 0$. 
Define $u = x_0 - y_0$.
Then $\Vert u \Vert = dist(x_0, Y)$, $dist(u, Y) = \Vert u \Vert$. The second equality holds because translating by an element of $Y$ does not change distance to $Y$.

**Step 3: Normalize to get distance 1**
Let $\omega = \frac{u}{\Vert u \Vert}$.
Then $\Vert \omega \Vert = 1$ and $dist(\omega, Y) = 1$.

**Step 4: Scale to arbitrary radius**
Given $r > 0$ define $x = y + r \omega$.
Then: $\Vert x - y \Vert = \Vert r \omega \Vert = r$ and $dist(x,Y) = dist(y + r \omega, Y)$.
Since $y \in Y$, translation invariance of distance gives $dist(y + r \omega, Y) = dist(r \omega, Y)$.
Using homogeneity of distance, $dist(r \omega, Y) = r dist(\omega, Y) = r$.
Since,  $dist(x,Y)= dist(r \omega, Y)$, $dist(x,Y) = r$.

Therefore, for every $y \in Y$ and every $r > 0$ there exists $x$ s.t. $\Vert x - y \Vert = dist(x, Y) = r$.

Notes
- The result shows that for every $y \in Y$ there are directions leaving $Y$ that behave like perpendicular directions: $\Vert x - y \Vert = dist(x,Y)$. In a Hilbert space that is trivial using orthogonality. In a general normed space there is no inner product - yet finite dimensionality allows you to recover a similar geometric property.

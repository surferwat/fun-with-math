Show that a normed space $(X, \Vert \cdot \Vert)$ is separable if and only if $X = \bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty} X_j$, where the $\{X_j\}$ are finite-dimensional subspaces of $X$.

$\mathit{Proof}$
$(\Rightarrow)$
If $X$ is separable, then $X = \bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty} X_j$.
Since $X$ is separable, there exists $A$, a countable dense subset of $X$.
So, for any $\epsilon > 0$, $x \in X$ there exists $a \in A$ s.t. $\Vert x - a \Vert < \epsilon$.
But $a \in A \subseteq \bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty} X_j$, so $\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty} X_j$ is dense (and with closure equals $X$).
 
$(\Leftarrow)$
If $X = \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} X_i$, then $X$ is separable.
$X_i \in \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} X_i$ is separable since $X_i$ is a subset of a separable $R^n$.
This means that $X_i$ has a countable dense subset.
Choose a countable dense subset $A_i \subset X_i$.
Define $A := \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} A_i$.
Let $x \in X$ s.t. $x \in X_i$.
Since $A_i$ is dense in $X_i$, for every $\epsilon > 0$, there exists $a_i \in A_i \subset A$ s.t. $\Vert x - a_i \Vert < \epsilon$.
Thus, $A$ is dense in $X$.
Therefore, $X$ has a countable dense subset, so $X$ is separable. $\blacksquare$

Notes:
- It says that in a separable normed space, you can “build up” the whole space from **countably many finite-dimensional pieces**.
- Conversely, if you can approximate every element of $X$ using vectors from a countable union of finite-dimensional spaces, then you can construct a **countable dense set**.
- So it connects a **topological property** (separability) with a **structural property** (countable union of finite-dimensional subspaces).

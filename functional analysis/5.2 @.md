Show that if $(V, \Vert \cdot \Vert_V)$ is a normed space, $W$ a vector space, and $T: W \to V$ a linear bijection, then $\Vert x \Vert_W := \Vert Tx \Vert_V$ defines a norm on $W$, and $T: (W, \Vert \cdot \Vert_W) \to (V, \Vert \cdot \Vert)$ is an isometry. (So $(W, \Vert \cdot \Vert_W)$ and $(V, \Vert \cdot \Vert_V)$ are isometrically isomorphic.)

$\it Proof$
**Step 1: $\Vert \cdot \Vert_W$ is a norm on $W$**
Define $\Vert x \Vert_W := \Vert Tx \Vert_V$ for all $x \in W$, where $T: W \to V$ is a linear bijection. 
We verify the three norm properties.
(i) Positivity and definiteness
For any $x \in W$, $\Vert x \Vert_W = \Vert Tx \Vert_V \ge 0$, since $\Vert \cdot \Vert_V$ is a norm. If $\Vert x \Vert_W = 0$ then $\Vert Tx \Vert_V = 0$, which implies $Tx = 0$. 
Because $T$ is injective, this implies $x=0$.
Conversely, if $x=0$, then $Tx = 0$ and $\Vert x \Vert_W = 0$.
(ii) Homogeneity
For any scalar $\alpha$ and $x \in W$, $\Vert \alpha x \Vert_W = \Vert T(\alpha x) \Vert_V = \Vert \alpha Tx \Vert_V = | \alpha | \Vert Tx \Vert_V = | \alpha | \Vert x \Vert_W$, using linearity of $T$ and homogeneity of $\Vert \cdot \Vert_V$.
(iii) Triangle inequality
For $x,y \in W$, $\Vert x + y \Vert_W = \Vert T(x+y) \Vert_V = \Vert Tx + Ty \Vert_V \le \Vert Tx \Vert_V + \Vert Ty \Vert_V = \Vert x \Vert_W + \Vert y \Vert_W$.
Since all three properties hold, $\Vert \cdot \Vert_W$ is a norm on $W$.

**Step 2: $T$ is an isometry**
For all $x \in W$, $\Vert Tx \Vert_V = \Vert x \Vert_W$ by definition of $\Vert \cdot \Vert_W$. 
Thus $T$ preserves norms, hence preserves distances: $\Vert Tx - Ty \Vert_V = \Vert T(x-y) \Vert_V = \Vert x - y \Vert_W$.
Therefore, $T: (W, \Vert \cdot \Vert_W) \to (V, \Vert \cdot \Vert/V)$ is an isometry.

The map $T$ is a linear bijective isometry, so $(W, \Vert \cdot \Vert_W)$ and $(V, \Vert \cdot \Vert_V)$ are isometrically isomorphic. $\blacksquare$

Notes:
- Definition 3.19 Two normed spaces $(X, \Vert \cdot \Vert_X)$ and $(Y, \Vert \cdot \Vert_Y)$ are said to be isomorphic if there exists a bijective linear map $T: X \to Y$ that satisfies (iii) and in this case we will write $X \cong Y$. In some cases we can strengthen (iii) and find a map $T:X \to Y$ that satisfies (i) a bijection, (ii) vector space linearity, and (iii') $T$ is an isometry ie we have $\Vert Tx \Vert_Y = \Vert x \Vert_X$ for all $x \in X$, so that $T$ preserves the norm (i.e., Y norm is just not comparable to X norm but rather is equal to). We will refer to such a map $T$ is an isometric isomorphism between the spaces $X$ and $Y$. (Pryce (1973) and some other authors use the term congruence in this case).

Show that the norms $\Vert \cdot \Vert_{l^1}$, $\Vert \cdot \Vert_{l^2}$, and $\Vert \cdot \Vert_{l^{\infty}}$ on $\mathbb{R^n}$ are all equivalent.

$\Vert \cdot \Vert_{l^1} = \sum_{j=1}^{n} | x_j |$.
$\Vert \cdot \Vert_{l^2} = (\sum_{j=1}^{n} | x_j |^2)^{1/2}$.
$\Vert \cdot \Vert_{l^{\infty}} = max_{j=1,...,n} | x_j |$.

WTS: there exists constants $0 \le \alpha_1 \le \alpha_2$ s.t. $\alpha_1 \Vert \cdot \Vert_{1} \le \Vert \cdot \Vert_{2} \le \alpha_2 \Vert \cdot \Vert_{1}$ for all $\cdot \in \mathbb{R^n}$.

For the lower bound, using Cauchy Schwarz, we have: 
$\Vert \cdot \Vert_{1} = \sum_{j=1}^{n} | x_j | \le \sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{n} | x_j |^2} \sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{n} 1^2} = (\sum_{j=1}^{n} | x_j |^2)^{1/2} \sqrt{n} = \Vert \cdot \Vert_{2}\sqrt{n}$.
$\Vert \cdot \Vert_{1} \le \Vert \cdot \Vert_{2}\sqrt{n}$.
$\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Vert \cdot \Vert_{1} \le \Vert \cdot \Vert_{2}$.

For the upper bound, using Lemma 3.10, we have:
$\Vert \cdot \Vert_{2} \le \Vert \cdot \Vert_{1}$.

Thus,  $0 \le \alpha_1=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \le \alpha_2=1$ s.t. $\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Vert \cdot \Vert_{1} \le \Vert \cdot \Vert_{2} \le \Vert \cdot \Vert_{1}$.
Thus, $\Vert \cdot \Vert_{1} \sim \Vert \cdot \Vert_{2}$.

WTS: there exists constants $0 \le \beta_1 \le \beta_2$ s.t. $\beta_1 \Vert \cdot \Vert_{\infty} \le \Vert \cdot \Vert_{2} \le \beta_2 \Vert \cdot \Vert_{\infty}$ for all $\cdot \in \mathbb{R^n}$.

For the lower bound, using Lemma 3.10, we have:
$\Vert \cdot \Vert_{\infty} \le \Vert \cdot \Vert_{2}$.

For the upper bound:
$\Vert \cdot \Vert_{2} = (\sum_{j=1}^{n} | x_j |^2)^{1/2} \le ((\sum_{j=1}^{n} | x_j |)^2)^{1/2} = \sum_{j=1}^{n} | x_j | \le n \cdot max_{j=1,...,n} | x_j | = n \Vert \cdot \Vert_{\infty}$.
$\Vert \cdot \Vert_{2} \le n \Vert \cdot \Vert_{\infty}$.

Thus,  $0 \le \beta_1=1 \le \beta_2=n$ s.t. $\Vert \cdot \Vert_{\infty} \le \Vert \cdot \Vert_{2} \le n \Vert \cdot \Vert_{\infty}$.
Thus, $\Vert \cdot \Vert_{2} \sim \Vert \cdot \Vert_{\infty}$.

Since $\Vert \cdot \Vert_{1} \sim \Vert \cdot \Vert_{2}$ and $\Vert \cdot \Vert_{2} \sim \Vert \cdot \Vert_{\infty}$,  using Lemma 3.17, we have $|| \cdot ||_1 \sim || \cdot ||_{\infty}$.
Therefore, the norms $\Vert \cdot \Vert_{1}$, $\Vert \cdot \Vert_{2}$, and $\Vert \cdot \Vert_{\infty}$ on $\mathbb{R^n}$ are all equivalent.

Notes
- Equivalence of norms ensures that they give rise to the same open sets and therefore make the same sequences convergent.
- Definition 3.16: Two norms $\Vert \cdot \Vert_1$ and $\Vert \cdot \Vert_2$ are equivalent if there exist constants $0 < c_1 \le c_2$ s.t $c_1 \Vert x \Vert_{1} \le \Vert x \Vert_2 \le c_2 \Vert x \Vert_1$ for all $x \in X$.
- Lemma 3.10: if $1 \le p \le q \le \infty$, then $l^p(\mathbb{K}) \subset l^q(\mathbb{K})$ and $\Vert x \Vert_{l^q} \le \Vert x \Vert_{l^p}$ whenever $x \in l^p(\mathbb{K})$.
- Lemma 3.17: Suppose that $\Vert \cdot \Vert_1$, $\Vert \cdot \Vert_2$, and $\Vert \cdot \Vert_3$ are all norms on a vector space $V$, such that $|| \cdot ||_1 \sim || \cdot ||_2$ and $|| \cdot ||_2 \sim || \cdot ||_3$. Then $|| \cdot ||_1 \sim || \cdot ||_3$.
- We can derive $\Vert \cdot \Vert_{2} \le \Vert \cdot \Vert_{1}$ explicitly: $\Vert \cdot \Vert_{2}^2 = ((\sum_{j=1}^{n} | x_j |^2)^{1/2})^{2} \implies \sum_{j=1}^{n} | x_j |^2 \le (\sum_{j=1}^{n} | x_j |)^2 =\Vert \cdot \Vert_{1}^2 \implies \Vert \cdot \Vert_{2}^2 \le \Vert \cdot \Vert_{1}^2$.
- This exercise demonstrates that we are free to choose the most convenient norm for the problem since this means that convergence, continuity, boundedness, and openess are independent of the choice of norm on $\mathbb{R}^n$.

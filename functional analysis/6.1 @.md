Using the upper and lower bounds on the factorial $\sqrt{2 \pi}n^{n+1/2} e^{-n} \le n! \le e n^{n+1/2}e^{-n}$ (see e.g. Holland, 2016) show that $\binom{2k}{k} \le c \frac{2^{2k}}{\sqrt{k}}$ where $c$ is a constant that does not depend on $k$.

**Step 1: Express the binomial coefficient**
$\binom{2k}{k} = \frac{(2k)!}{(k!)^2}$
To obtain an upper bound, we use the upper bound for $(2k)!$, the lower bound for $k!$. 

**Step 2: Apply the factorial bounds**
For the numerator: $(2k)! \le e(2k)^{2k+\frac{1}{2}}e^{-2k}$.
For the denominator: $k! \ge \sqrt{2 \pi}k^{k+\frac{1}{2}} e^{-k} \Rightarrow (k!)^2 \ge 2\pi k^{2k+1} e^{-2k}$.

**Step 3: Form the quotient**
$\binom{2k}{k}\le \frac{e(2k)^{2k+\frac{1}{2}}e^{-2k}}{2 \pi k^{2k+1} e^{-2k}} = \frac{e}{2 \pi}\frac{(2k)^{2k+\frac{1}{2}}}{k^{2k+1}}$.

**Step 4: Simplify the powers**
Write $(2k)^{2k+\frac{1}{2}} = (2k)^{2k}(2k)^{1/2} = 2^{2k} k^{2k} \sqrt{2k}$.
Therefore, $\frac{(2k)^{2k+\frac{1}{2}}}{k^{2k+1}} = 2^{2k} \frac{\sqrt{2k}}{{k}} = 2^{2k}\frac{\sqrt 2}{\sqrt k}$.
Hence $\binom{2k}{k} \le \frac{e}{2\pi} \cdot 2^{2k} \cdot \frac{\sqrt 2}{\sqrt k}$.

**Step 5: Final form**
Set $c=\frac{e\sqrt{2}}{2\pi}$
Then $\binom{2k}{k} \le c \frac{2^{2k}}{\sqrt k}$ where $c$ is a constant independent of $k$.

Notes
- This tells us the exponential growth is $4^k$ and there is a subexponential correction of order $k^{-1/2}$. The $\sqrt k$ term is not cosmetic - it is essential. It tells us the central binomial coefficient is much smaller than $4^k$ but only by a polynomial factor.
- In functional analysis and complex analysis, expressions involving $\binom{2k}{k}$ appear in power series such as: $\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \binom{2k}{k} x^k$. Since $\binom{2k}{k} \sim \frac{4^k}{\sqrt \pi k}$, the root test gives: $lim \, sup_{k \to \infty} \sqrt{k} \binom{2k}{k} = 4$. So the radius of convergence is $R=\frac{1}{4}$.  This type of estimate is exactly what you need when studying: generating functions, analytic function expansions, spectral radius calculations, operator power series.

Use the Weierstrass Approximation Theorem to prove Lemma 6.7. [Hint: approximate |x| uniformly on [-1,1] using polynomials.]

Lemma 6.7 Suppose that $X$ is compact and that $A$ is a subalgebra of $C(X; \mathbb{R})$. If $f,g \in A$, then $max(f,g) \in \bar{A}$ and $min(f,g) \in \bar{A}$.

Let $X$ be compact and let $A \subset C(X; \mathbb{R})$. Suppose $f,g \in A$. We prove that $max(f,g)$, $min(f,g) \in \bar{A}$ (the closure take in the uniform norm).

**Step 1: Express max/min using the absolute value**
For real numbers $a$, $b$, $max(a,b) = \frac{a+b+|a-b|}{2}$, $min(a,b) = \frac{a+b-|a-b|}{2}$.
Hence for functions $f,g$, $max(f,g) = \frac{f+g+|f-g|}{2}$, $min(f,g) = \frac{f+g-|f-g|}{2}$.
Since $f,g \in A$ and $A$ is closed under addition and scalar multiplication, we have $f+g$, $f-g \in A$.
Thus the problem reduces to showing $|f - g| \in \bar A$.

**Step 2: Reduce to approximating $|x|$**
Let $h=f-g$.
Then $h \in A$. Since $X$ is compact and $h$ is continuous, $h$ is bounded, so there exist $M > 0$ s.t. $|h(x)| \le M$ for all $x \in X$.
Define $k(x) = \frac{h(x)}{M}$.
Then $k \in A$ and $k(X) \subset [-1,1]$.

**Step 3: Approximate $|x|$ on $[-1,1]$**
By the Weierstrass Approximation Theorem, there exist polynomials $p_n$ s.t. $sup_{t \in [-1,1]} | p_n(t) - |t|| \to 0$.

**Step 4: Construct approximating functions**
Define $F_n(x) = M p_n(k(x))$.
Each polynomial has the form $p_n(t) = a_0 + a_1t + a_2t^2 + ... + a_m t^m$.
Substituting $k(x)$ gives $p_n(k(x)) = a_0 + a_1k(x) + a_2k(x)^2 + ... + a_mk(x)^m$.
Thus, each $p_n(k(x))$ is obtained by adding multiples of powers of $k$. 
Since $k \in A$, repeated multiplication shows that $k^2$,$k^3$, ..., $k^m \in A$.
Since $A$ is closed under addition and scalar multiplication, it follows that $p_n(k(x)) \in A$.
Hence, we have $F_n \in A$.

**Step 5: Show uniform convergence**
Since $F_n(x) = M p_n(k(x))$, $F_n(x) - | h(x) | = Mp_n(k(x)) - |h(x)|$.
Since $h(x)=Mk(x)$, $|h(x)| = M|k(x)|$.
Thus, $F_n(x) - |h(x)| = Mp_n(k(x)) - M|k(x)| = M(p_n(k(x)) - |k(x)|)$.
Taking absolute values, $| F_n(x) - |h(x)|| = |M(p_n(k(x)) - |k(x)|)|$.
Since $M > 0$, $| F_n(x) - |h(x)|| = M|(p_n(k(x)) - |k(x)|)|$.
Since $k(x) \in [-1,1]$, $sup_{x \in X} |F_n(x) - |h(x)|| \le M sup_{t \in [-1,1]} |p_n^-(t)-|t||$.
The right-hand side tends to $0$, so $F_n \to |h|$ uniformly on $X$.
Since each $F_n \in A$, it follows that $|h| = |f-g| \in \bar{A}$.

**Step 6: Conclusion
Using $max(f,g) = \frac{f+g+|f-g|}{2}$, $min(f,g) = \frac{f+g-|f-g|}{2}$, and the facts that $f,g \in A$ and $|f-g| \in \bar{A}$, we conclude that $max(f,g)$, $min(f,g) \in \bar{A}$.

Notes
- To approximate $|h(x)|$, we scale back: $F_n(x) = Mp_n(k(x))$. Then $F_n(x) \approx M |k(x)| = M (\frac{|h(x)|}{M}) = | h(x) |$.
- We want to show that $F_n(x) = Mp_n(k(x)) \to |h(x)|$ uniformly on $X$. Uniform convergence is measured by the sup norm: $sup_{x \in X} | F_n(x) - |h(x)| | \to 0$. So naturally, we look at the difference $F_n(x) - |h(x)|$.

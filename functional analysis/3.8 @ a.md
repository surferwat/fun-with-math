Show that if $U$ is an open subspace of a normed space $X$ then $U=X$. (Naylor and Sell, 1982)

Let $u_0 \in U$ with $u_0 \ne 0$.
Since $U$ is open, there exists $\epsilon > 0$ s.t. $B(u_0, \epsilon)$ is contained in $U$.
Since $U$ is a vector space in which $u_0 \in U$ and $B(u_0, \epsilon) \subseteq U$, we can translate this ball to center at $0$.
Let $B(0, \epsilon) = \{ x' \in X \, | \, \Vert x' \Vert < \epsilon \}$.
Choose $x' \in B(0, \epsilon)$ and define $u = u_0 + x'$.
Then $u - u_0 = x' \in U$.
So, we have established that $x' \in B(0, \epsilon) \subseteq U$.
Choose arbitrarily $x \in X$ with $x \ne 0$. 
Let $x = (\frac{\epsilon}{2\Vert x \Vert})^{-1}\frac{\epsilon}{2\Vert x \Vert}x$.
Then $\Vert \frac{\epsilon}{2\Vert x \Vert}x \Vert$ = $\frac{\epsilon}{2\Vert x \Vert}\Vert x \Vert = \frac{\epsilon}{2} < \epsilon$, so $\frac{\epsilon}{2\Vert x \Vert}x \in B(0, \epsilon)$.
Since $U$ is closed under scalar multiplication, $x=(\frac{\epsilon}{2\Vert x \Vert})^{-1}(\frac{\epsilon}{2\Vert x \Vert}x) \in U$.
So, every $x \in X$ belongs to $U$, so $X \subseteq U$.
Since $U \subseteq X$ and $X \subseteq U$, therefore, $U=X$.

Notes
- Openness implies existence of an open ball entirely contained in $U$.
- Translating the open ball at $u_0$​ to the origin leverages vector space closure under addition.
- The open ball centered at zero is contained in $U$ due to translation invariance in vector spaces.
- Scaling any vector $x$ to fit inside the ball uses norm homogeneity and scalar multiplication closure.
- Scaling back the vector from the smaller vector inside the ball recovers $x$ inside $U$.
- Arbitrariness of $x \in X$ ensures $U$ contains all of $X$, so $U=X$.

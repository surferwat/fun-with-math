Show that if $f : [a,b] \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is $C^2$ on $(a, b)$ and $C^1$ on $[a,b]$, then $f$ is convex on $[a,b]$ if $f''(x) \ge 0$. Deduce that $f(x)=e^x$ and $f(x)=|x|^p$, $1 \le p \le \infty$, are convex functions on $\mathbb{R}$.

Proof
Pick two points $x,y \in [a,b]$ s.t. $x < y$.
Reparameterize $f$ on the interval from $x$ to $y$, $\phi(t) := f(x+t(y-x))$ for $t \in [0,1]$.
Derive $\phi''(t)$.
$\phi'(t) := \frac{d}{dt}[f(x+t(y-x))]=(y-x)f'(x+t(y-x))$.
$\phi''(t) := \frac{d}{dt}[(y-x)f'(x+t(y-x) ]$
$= (y-x) [\frac{d}{dt} f'(x+t(y-x))]$
$= (y-x) [f''(x+t(y-x)) (y-x)]$
$= (y-x)^2 f''(x+t(y-x))$.
Thus, $\phi''(t) = (y-x)^2 f''(x+t(y-x))$.
 $(y-x)^2 \ge 0$ and by assumption, $f''(x) \ge 0 \rightarrow \phi''(t) \ge 0$ for all $t \in (0,1)$.
Thus, $\phi(t)$ is convex on $[0,1]$.
Since $\phi(t)$ is convex, $\phi(t)$ satisfies: $\phi(\lambda) \le (1-\lambda)\phi(0) + \lambda \phi(1)$.
Substituting $\phi$ for $f$: $f((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)) \le (1-\lambda)f(x) + \lambda f(y)$ which is the definition of convexity.
Thus, $f$ is convex on $[a,b]$. QED.

Let $f(x)=e^x$.
Reparameterize $f$ on the interval from $x$ to $y$, $\phi(t) := e^{x+t(y-x)}$ for $t \in [0,1]$.
Let $u(t) = x+t(y-x)$.
Then $\phi'(t) = \frac{d}{dt}e^{u(t)}=e^{u(t)}u'(t)=e^{x+t(y-x)}(y-x)$.
Then $\phi''(t) = (y-x)^2e^{x+t(y-x)}$.
Since $(y-x)^2 \ge 0$ and $e^{x+t(y-x)} \ge 0$, $\phi''(t) \ge 0$ for all $t \in [0,1] \rightarrow \phi(t)$ is convex.
It follows, as we have shown in the proof, $e^x$ is convex.

Let $f(x)=|x|^p$.
Reparameterize $f$ on the interval from $x$ to $y$, $\phi(t):=|x+t(y-x)|^p$ for $t \in[0,1]$.
Let $u(t) = x+t(y-x)$.
Then $\phi'(t) = \frac{d}{dt} |u(t)|^p = p|u(t)|^{(p-1)}\frac{d}{dt}|u(t)| = (y-x) p | x+t(y-x) |^{(p-1)}$.
Then $\phi''(t) = (y-x)^2 p(p-1)|x+t(y-x)|^{p-2}$.
Since $(y-x)^2 \ge 0$, $p(p-1) \ge 0$, and $x + t(y-x) \ge 0$, $\phi''(t) \ge 0$ for all $t \in [0,1] \rightarrow \phi(t)$ is convex.
It follows, as we have shown in the proof, $|x|^p$ for $1 \le p \le \infty$ is convex.


Notes
$C^k$ refers to class of functions that have continuous derivatives up to order $k$.
$f : [a,b] \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is convex if whenever $x,y \in [a, b]$ we have $f(\lambda x + (1 - \lambda) y) \le \lambda f(x) + (1-\lambda)f(y)$ for all $\lambda \in (0,1)$
Since $f''(x) \ge0$, the slope of $f(x)$ is non-decreasing so $f'(x)$ is increasing or constant, a monotonic non-decreasing function.

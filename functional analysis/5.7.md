Use the result of the previous exercise to show that no infinite-dimensional Banach space can have a countable Hamel basis. (Megginson, 1998) Hint: given a Hamel basis ${e_j}_{j=1}^{\infty}$ for $X$, let $X_n = Span(e_1, ..., e_n)$. Now find a sequence $(y_n)$ with $y_n \in X_n$ s.t. $\Vert y_n - y_{n-1} \Vert = dist(y_n, X_{n-1})= 3^{-n}$ and show that $(y_n)$ is Cauchy in $X$ but its limit cannot lie in any of $X_n$. For another, simpler, proof using the Baire Category Theorem, see Exercise 22.1.

Let $X$ be an infinite-dimensional Banach space. Suppose, for contradiction, that $X$ has a countable Hamel basis $\{e_j\}_{j=1}^{\infty}$. 

Define $X_n := span(e_1, ..., e_n)$.

Each $X_n$ is finite-dimensional, hence closed, and since the basis is Hamel, $X = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} X_n$

**Step 1: Construct a special sequence**
Using the result of the previous exercise. If $M$ is a proper closed subspace of a Banach space $X$, then for any $\epsilon > 0$ there exists $x \in X$ such that $dist(x,M) = \epsilon$. 
Since $X_{n-1}$ is a proper closed subspace of $X_n$, we may inductively choose $y_n \in X_n$ s.t. $\Vert y_n - y_{n-1} \Vert = dist(y_n, X_{n-1}) = 3^{-n}$, with $y_1 \in X_1$ satisfying $\Vert y_1 \Vert = 3^{-1}$. 

**Step 2: $(y_n)$ is Cauchy**
For $m > n$, $y_m - y_n = \sum_{k=n+1}^{m} (y_k - y_{k-1}) = \Vert \sum_{k=n+1}^{m} (y_k - y_{k-1}) \Vert \le \sum_{k=n+1}^{m} \Vert (y_k - y_{k-1}) \Vert = \sum_{k=n+1}^{m} 3^{-k}$, so $\Vert y_m - y_n \Vert \le \sum_{k=n+1}^{m} 3^{-k}$. 
Since $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} 3^{-k}$ converges, the tail sums tend to zero. Hence $(y_n)$ is Cauchy. 
Because $X$ is complete, there exists $y := lim_{n \to \infty} y_n \in X$.

**Step 3: The limit cannot lie in any $X_n$**
Fix $N$. For all $n > N$, $y_n \in X_n$, $dist(y_n, X_n) = \sum_{k=N+1}^{n} 3^{-k}$.
Passing to the limit as $n \to \infty$, $dist(y, X_n) = \sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty} 3^{-k} > 0$.
Thus $y \notin X_n$. 
Since $N$ was arbitrary, $y \notin \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} X_n$

**Step 4: Contradiction**
But we assumed $X = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} X_n$.
This contradiction shows that $X$ cannot have a countable Hamel basis.

**Conclusion**
No infinite-dimensional Banach space can possess a countable Hamel basis.

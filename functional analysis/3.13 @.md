If $(X, \Vert \cdot \Vert_X) \simeq (Y, \Vert \cdot \Vert_Y)$ show that $X$ is separable if and only if $Y$ is separable.

$\mathit{Proof}$
Let $x = (x_n) \in X$.
Let $y = (y_n) \in Y$.
Assume $X$ is separable. 
Then there exists $A$ , a countable dense subset of $X$ s.t. $a = (a_n) \in A$ s.t. for every $\epsilon > 0$, $\Vert x - a\Vert_X  < \epsilon$.
Since $(X, \Vert \cdot \Vert_X) \simeq (Y, \Vert \cdot \Vert_Y)$, there exists a linear bijection $T: X \to Y$ and $T^{-1}:Y \to X$.
Since $A$ is a countable subset, $B = T(A)$ is a countable subset of $Y$.
We show that $B$ is dense.
Since T is linear and bounded, there exists $c > 0$ s.t. $\Vert Ty \Vert_Y \le c \Vert x \Vert_X$
Fix any $y \in Y$. 
Let $x := T^{-1}y \in X$. Since $A$ is dense in $X$, for any $\epsilon > 0$ there is $a \in A$ with $\Vert x - a \Vert_X < \frac{\epsilon}{c}$.
$\implies c\Vert x-a \Vert_X \le \epsilon$
$\implies \Vert T(x-a) \Vert_Y \le c\Vert x-a \Vert_X \le \epsilon$
$\implies \Vert y-b \Vert_Y = \Vert Tx-Ta \Vert_Y=\Vert T(x-a) \Vert_Y \le c\Vert x-a \Vert_X \le \epsilon$
$\implies \Vert y-b \Vert_Y \le \epsilon$.
So, every $y \in Y$ can be approximated by elements of $B$.
Since $y$ was chosen arbitrarily, $B=Y$, so $B$ is dense.
Thus, $B$ is a countable dense subset $\implies Y$ is separable.
The converse (if $Y$ is separable then $X$ is separable) is identical, using $T^{-1}$ in place of $T$. $\blacksquare$

Notes
- Isomorphism of normed spaces (usually Banach space isomorphism) means there exists a bounded linear bijection with bounded inverse between two normed spaces.
- This exercise demonstrates that separability is preserved under such isomorphisms—that is, it's a topological property invariant under topological vector space isomorphisms.

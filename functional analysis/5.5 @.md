Show that if $Y$ is a subspace of a normed space $X$ and $x \in X$ \ $Y$, then  $dist(\alpha x, Y) = | \alpha | dist(x, Y)$ for any $\alpha \in \mathbb{K}$, and $dist(x + \omega, Y)=dist(x,Y)$ for any $\omega \in Y$.

Let $X$ be a normed space over $\mathbb{K}$ (either $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$) and let $Y \subset X$ be a subspace.
For $x \in X$ recall the definition of the distance from $x$ to $Y$:  $dist(x,Y) := \inf_{y \in Y} \Vert x - y \Vert$.
We prove the two properties separately.

Homogeneity
$dist(\alpha x, Y) = | \alpha | dist(x,Y)$ for any $\alpha \in \mathbb{K}$.
Case 1: $\alpha = 0$
Then $dist(0,Y) = 0$ since $0 \in Y$ (because $Y$ is a subspace).
Also, $|\alpha | dist(x,Y) = 0$.
So the identity holds.
Case 2: $\alpha \ne 0$
$dist(\alpha x, Y) = \inf_{y \in Y} \Vert \alpha x - y \Vert$.
Since $Y$ is a subspace, $y \in Y$ if and only if $y = \alpha z$ for some $z \in Y$.
(Indeed, $z = \alpha^{-1} y \in Y$.)
Thus we can rewrite the infimum: 
$dist(\alpha x , Y)$
$= inf_{z \in Y} \Vert \alpha x - \alpha z \Vert$.
$=  inf_{z \in Y} \alpha \Vert x - z \Vert$ Factor out $\alpha$
$= |\alpha| \, inf_{z \in Y} \Vert x - z \Vert$
$= | \alpha | \, dist(x,Y)$.

Translation by elements of $Y$:
$dist(x + \omega, Y) = dist(x, Y)$ for any $\omega \in Y$.
We compute: $dist(x + \omega, Y) = inf_{y \in Y} \Vert x + \omega - y \Vert$.
Since $Y$ is a subspace, for each $y \in Y$, $x + \omega - y = x - (y-\omega)$.
Because $y - \omega \in Y$, the set $\{y-\omega : y \in Y\}$ is just $Y$ again.
Therefore,  $dist(x + \omega, Y) = inf_{z \in Y} \Vert x - z \Vert = dist(x, Y)$.

Notes
- Let $X'$ be a vector space over $\mathbb{K}$.  A subset $Y \subset X$ is a subspace if and only if: $0 \in Y$; $y_1, y_2 \in Y \to y_1 + y_2 \in Y$; $y \in Y$, $\alpha \in \mathbb{K} \to \alpha y \in Y$ i.e., closure under scalar multiplication.
- This problem tells that distance to a subspace behaves exactly like a norm on the quotient space $X$ \ $Y$. That's the real significance.
- From $dist(x + \omega, Y) = dist(x, Y)$ $(\omega \in Y)$,, we learn: The distance does not change if you move inside the subspace. So the distance only depends on the equivalence class $x + Y$. That means the function $x \mapsto dist(x,Y)$ is a really a function on the quotient space $X / Y$ not on individual vectors.
- $dist(x,Y)$ is not just a random infimum - it is the natural norm of the coset $x + Y$.

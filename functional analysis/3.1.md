Show that if $d$ is a metric on a vector space $X$ derived from a norm $\left\lVert \cdot \right\rVert$, i.e. $d(x,y) = \left\lVert x-y \right\rVert$, then $d$ is translation invariant and homogeneous, i.e., $d(x+z, y+z) = d(x,y)$ and $d(\alpha x, \alpha y) = \alpha d(x,y)$. Deduce that the metric on $s$ in Exercise 2.4 does not come from a norm (Kreyszig, 1978).

$d(x+z, y+z) = \left\lVert (x+z) - (y+z) \right\rVert = \left\lVert x + z - y - z \right\rVert = \left\lVert x - y \right\rVert = d(x,y).$
$d(\alpha x, \alpha y) = \left\lVert \alpha x - \alpha y \right\rVert = \left\lVert \alpha (x-y) \right\rVert = | \alpha | \left\lVert x - y \right\rVert = | \alpha | d(x,y)$.
Therefore, $d$ is translation invariant and homogenous.

$d(\alpha x, \alpha y) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} 2^{-j} \frac{| \alpha x_j - \alpha y_j |}{1 + | \alpha x_j - \alpha y_j |} = \sum_{j=1}^{inf} 2^{-j} \frac{|\alpha||(x_j - y_j) |}{1 + |\alpha|| (x_j - y_j) |}$ 
$|\alpha| d(x,y) = |\alpha| \sum_{j=1}^{inf} 2^{-j} \frac{| (x_j - y_j) |}{1 + | (x_j - y_j) |}$
$\sum_{j=1}^{inf} 2^{-j} \frac{|\alpha||(x_j - y_j) |}{1 + |\alpha|| (x_j - y_j) |} \ne |\alpha| \sum_{j=1}^{inf} 2^{-j} \frac{| (x_j - y_j) |}{1 + | (x_j - y_j) |}$ unless $|\alpha|=1$.
Therefore, $d(\alpha x, \alpha y) \ne |\alpha| d(x,y)$.
Since the metric on $s$ is not homogenous, it cannot not come from a norm.

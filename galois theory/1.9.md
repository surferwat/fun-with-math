The polynomial $P$ of the preceding exercise is called the resultant of $x^3+bx^2+cx+d$ and $x^3+Bx^2+Cx+D$. This gives the resultant of $x^3+(b/a)x^2+(c/a)x+(d/a)$ and $x^3+(B/A)x^2+(C/A)x+(D/A)$ which, because these polynomials have the same roots, it is natural to call the resultant of $ax^3+bx^2+cx+d$ and $Ax^3+Bx^2+Cx+D$. Find this resultant. [Easy from Exercise 8.] This, in essence, is the form in which Newton gives the answer. Prove that the resultant is $0$ if and only if the two polynomials have a root in common $A=a=0$ i.e., neither polynomial is in fact cubic.

Let $P(b,c,d,B,C,D)$ be the resultant of $x^3+bx^2+cx+d$ and $x^3+Bx^2+Cx+D$. From exercise $1.8$, we know that: 
$P(b,c,d,B,C,D)$
$= -d^3 + Bcd^2 - B^2bd^2 + B^3d^2 - Cdc^2 + 2Cbd^2 + BCbcd - 3BCd^2 - B^2Ccd - C^2b^2d + 2C^2cd$
$+ BC^2bd - C^3d + Dc^3 - 3Dbcd + 3Dd^2 - BDbc^2 + 2BDb^2d + BDcd + B^2Dc^2 - 2B^2Dbd + CDb^2c$
$- 2CDc^2 - CDbd - BCDbc + 3BCDd+ C^2Dc - D^2b^3 + 3D^2bc - 3D^2d + BD^2b^2 - 2BD^2c - CD^2b$
$+ D^3$.

Let $P(a,b,c,d,A,B,C,D)$ be the resultant of $ax^3+bx^2+cx+d$ and $Ax^3+Bx^2+Cx+D$. We devise  working the leading coefficients $A$ and $a$ into $P(b,c,d,B,C,D)$.
$P(a,b,c,d,A,B,C,D)$
$= -A^3d^3 + A^2Bcd^2 - AB^2bd^2 + B^3ad^2 - A^2Cdc^2 + 2A^2Cbd^2 + ABCbcd - 3ABCad^2 - B^2Cacd$
$- AC^2b^2d+ 2AC^2acd + BC^2abd - C^3a^2d + A^2Dc^3 - 3A^2Dbcd + 3A^2Dad^2 - ABDbac^2 + 2ABDb^2d$
$+ ABDacd+ B^2Dac^2 - 2B^2Dabd+ ACDb^2c - 2ACDac^2 - ACDabd - BCDabc + 3BCDa^2d$
$+ C^2Da^2c - AD^2b^3 + 3AD^2abc- 3AD^2a^2d + BD^2ab^2 - 2BD^2a^2c - CD^2a^2b + D^3a^3$.

$\mathit{Proof}$
$(\Rightarrow)$
Suppose $A \ne a \ne 0$ (i.e., the two polynomials don't have a root in common).
So, both polynomials are degree $3$.
By definition, the resultant of two cubic polynomials is a specific polynomial expression in their coefficients (here, $a$,$b$,$c$,$d$,$A$,$B$,$C$,$D$).
Looking at the resultant $P$, each term contains either $A$ or $a$, so if both $A$ and $a$ are nonzero, then none of the terms in $P$ vanish by themselves. That means $P$ should not be zero unless there is a rare case of a cancellation of terms.
But, by assumption, $P=0$.
The only way $P=0$ is in which $A=a=0$.

$(\Leftarrow)$
Since each term has an $A$ or $a$, each term will evaluate to $0$.
The sum of $0$ is $0$.
Thus, $P(a,b,c,d,A,B,C,D)=0$. 

Therefore, the resultant is $0$ if and only if the two polynomials have a root in common $A=a=0$ i.e., neither polynomial is in fact cubic. $\blacksquare$

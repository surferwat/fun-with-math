Prove Dedekind's "reciprocity theorem" of Galois theory (1855, first published in 1982, [S1]): Let $f$ and $g$ be irreducible polynomials with coefficients in $K$. Let $f$ decompose into irreducible factors $f=f_1f_2 \dots f_{\mu}$ over $K(b)$ where $b$ is a root of $g$. Similarly, let $g$ decompose $g= g_1g_2\dots g$, over $K(a)$ where $a$ is a root of $f$. Then $\mu = v$ and the $g_1$ can be reordered in such a way that the ratio of $\operatorname{deg} f_k$ to $\operatorname{deg} g_k$ is the same for all $k=1,2, \dots \mu$. (Show that $a_i$ and $a_j$ are roots of the same $f_k$ if and only if there is an $S$ in the Galois group of $fg=0$ over $K$ with $Sa_i = a_j$ and $Sb_1=b_1$ where $b=b_1$ is a fixed root of $g$. Fix a root $a_1$ of $f$ and define $g_k$ to be the factor of $g$ over $K(a_1)$ of which $S^{-1}b_1$ is a root, where $S$ is in the Galois group of $fg=0$ over $K$ and $Sa_1$ is a root of $f_k$. Show that $\operatorname{deg} f_k/\operatorname{deg} f = \operatorname{deg} g_k/\operatorname{deg} g$ by showing both are the proportion of $S$'s carrying $a_1$ to a root of $f_k$ (or $b_1$ to a root of $g_k$).

Let $L$ be the splitting field over $K$ of $fg$, and let $G$ be the Galois group of $L/K$. 
Fix $b_1=b$ and a root $a_1$ of $f$. 
The subgroup of $G$ fixing $b_1$ is the Galois group of $L/K(b_1)$, and hence, by transitivity, it acts transitively on the roots of each irreducible factor $f_k$ of $f$ over $K(b_1)$. 
It follows that $a_i$ and $a_j$ are roots of the same $f_k$ if and only if there is an $S\in G$ such that $Sa_i=a_j$ and $Sb_1=b_1$. 
Indeed, the condition is clearly necessary by the preceding transitivity, and every element fixing $b_1$ carries each $f_k$ into itself. 

Now fix $a_1$, and for each $S\in G$ with $Sa_1$ a root of $f_k$, let $g_k$ be the irreducible factor of $g$ over $K(a_1)$ having $S^{-1}b_1$ as a root. 
We must first show that this rule assigns different indices to different factors - if $S_1 a_1, S_2a_1$ are roots of the same $f_k$, then $S_1^{-1}b_1, S_2^{-1}b_1$ belong to the same $g_j$.
Suppose that $S_1$ and $S_2$ give the same factor of $g$ means that $S_1^{-1}b_1$ and $S_2^{-1}b_1$ are roots of the same irreducible factor $g_j$ of $g$ over $K(a_1)$.
The goal is to prove that this happens exactly when $S_1a_1$ and $S_2a_1$ are roots of the same factor $f_k$.
By the corresponding characterization over $K(a_1)$, there is a $T_2\in G$ with $T_2a_1=a_1$ and $T_2S_1^{-1}b_1=S_2^{-1}b_1$. 
Put $T_1=S_2T_2S_1^{-1}$. Then $T_1b_1=b_1$ and $T_1S_1a_1=S_2a_1$, so $S_1a_1$ and $S_2a_1$ are roots of the same $f_k$.
Conversely, if $S_1a_1$ and $S_2a_1$ are roots of the same $f_k$, there is a $T_1\in G$ with $T_1b_1=b_1$ and $T_1S_1a_1=S_2a_1$; putting $T_2=S_2^{-1}T_1S_1$ gives $T_2a_1=a_1$ and $T_2S_1^{-1}b_1=S_2^{-1}b_1$. 
Thus the rule assigns exactly one $g_k$ to each $f_k$, and, since every factor of $g$ over $K(a_1)$ occurs in this way, it gives a bijection between the factors. 
Hence $\mu=v$. 

It remains to compare the degrees. 
Since $G$ acts transitively on the roots of $f$, the number of elements $S\in G$ carrying $a_1$ to any particular root $a_j$ is independent of $j$; call it $N$. 
Therefore $\vert G \vert=N\deg f$. 
Similarly, the number of elements $S\in G$ carrying $a_1$ to a root of $f_k$ is $N\deg f_k$, so the proportion of elements of $G$ carrying $a_1$ to a root of $f_k$ is $\deg f_k/\deg f$. 
By the definition of $g_k$, $Sa_1$ is a root of $f_k$ if and only if $S^{-1}b_1$ is a root of $g_k$. 
Since inversion is a bijection of $G$, this is also the proportion of elements $S\in G$ for which $S^{-1}b_1$ is a root of $g_k$. 
The group $G$ acts transitively on the roots of $g$, so if $M$ is the number of elements carrying $b_1$ to any specified root of $g$, then $\vert G \vert=M\deg g$, and the number carrying $b_1$ to a root of $g_k$ is $M\deg g_k$.
Thus the same proportion is $\deg g_k/\deg g$. 
Consequently $\frac{\deg f_k}{\deg f}=\frac{\deg g_k}{\deg g}$ for every $k$, which proves Dedekind's reciprocity theorem.

Author's solution
By Exercise 4 of the Fifth Set, the Galois group of $fg=0$ over $K(b_1)$ acts transitively on the roots of $f_j$. By Exercise 9, this Galois group contains the substitutions of the Galois group of $fg=0$ over $K$ that leave $b_1$ fixed. Conversely, any such substitution carries roots of $f_k$ to roots of $f_k$. The suggested rule assigns at least one index $k=1,2, \dots, \mu$ to each factor of $g$ over $K(a_1)$, and assigns each index to at least one factor. It needs to be shown that it never assigns different indices to the same factor. By the preceding, this amounts to showing that if $S_1$ and $S_2$ are given then there is a $T_1$ with $T_1b_1=b_1$ and $T_1S_1a_1=S_2a_1$ if and only if there is a $T_2$ with $T_2a_1=a_1$ and $T_2S_1^{-1}b_1=S_2^{-1}b_1$. This follows from the formula $T_2 = S_2^{-1}T_1S_1$. Since the $S$'s act transitively on the $a$'s (Exercise 4 of the Fifth Set) the number of $S$'s that carry $a_1$ to $a_j$ is the same for all $j$, call it $N$. Then $N \cdot \operatorname{deg} f=$ number of $S$'s and $N\cdot \operatorname{deg} f_k =$ number of $S$'s that carry $a_1$ to a root of $f_k$. But $Sa_1$ is a root of $f_k$ if and only if $S^{-1}b_1$ is a root of $g_k$. Thus $\operatorname{deg} f_k / \operatorname{deg} f =$ proportion of $S$'s that carry $a_1$ to a root of $f_k =$ proportion of $S$'s such that $S^{-1}b_1$ is a root of $g_k = \operatorname{deg} g_k/ \operatorname{deg} g$.

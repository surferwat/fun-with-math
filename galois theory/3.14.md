Find a ruler and compass construction for the square root, or, as Euclid would express it, given a rectangle, construct a square with the same area. (Euclid's Elements, Book II, Proposition 14)

This is a classic geometric construction from Euclid's Elements (Book II, Proposition 14), and it gives a ruler-and-compass method to construct $\sqrt(ab)$ - i.e., a square with the same area as a rectangle of sides $a$ and $b$.

**Step 1: Lay out a line segment**
Draw a straight line and mark two adjacent segments on it:
- $AB = a$
- $BC = b$
So the total segment $AC = a + b$.

**Step 2: Find the midpoint**
Construct the midpoint $M$ of $AC$.

**Step 3: Draw a semicircle**
With center $M$ and radius $MA$, draw a semicircle over segment $AC$.

**Step 4: Erect a perpendicular**
At point $B$, construct a line perpendicular at point $D$.
Let this perpendicular intersect the semicircle at point $D$.

**Step 5: Result**
The segment $BD$ has length $BD = \sqrt ab$.
So a square with side $BD$ has area equal to the original rectangle.

**Why this works**
We look at three triangles $ADC$, $ADB$, $BDC$.
All three triangles are similar because 
- they all have right angles
- they share acute angles
- so $ADB ~ ADC ~ BDC$
$ADB$ and $ADC$ are similar so $BD/AB = BC/BD$ so $BD^2 = AB \cdot BC = ab$ so $BD = \sqrt ab$.

Notes
- Start with a rectangle of side lengths $a$ and $b$. Its area is $ab$. If you construct a square with the same area, and call its side $s$, then $s^2 = ab$. Therefore: $s = \sqrt ab$. We are essentially converting a rectangle (area ab) into a square, whose side length is $\sqrt ab$.

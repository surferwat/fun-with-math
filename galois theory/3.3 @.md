Derive the formulas for the 5th roots of unity given in $\S 21$. Use them (preferably with a pocket calculator) to calculate $sin 72^{\circ}$ and $cos 72^{\circ}$ and check the result.

**Derive the formulas**
5th roots of unity given in $\S 21$
$\alpha = \frac{\sqrt 5 - 1 \pm \sqrt{-2\sqrt 5 - 10}}{4}$, $\frac{-\sqrt 5 - 1 \pm \sqrt{2\sqrt 5 - 10}}{4}$

$\alpha^5 = 1 \Rightarrow \alpha^5 - 1 = 0 \Rightarrow \alpha^5 - 1 = (\alpha-1)(\alpha^4+\alpha^3+\alpha^2+\alpha+1) = 0$.
Thus, the non-trivial 5th roots of unity satisfy $\alpha^4+\alpha^3 + \alpha^2 + \alpha + 1 = 0$.

Use symmetry and divide by $\alpha^2$ since roots come in inverse pairs: $\alpha^2 + \alpha + 1 + \alpha^{-1} + \alpha^{-2} = 0$.
Group terms: $(\alpha + \alpha^{-1}) + (\alpha^2 + \alpha^{-2}) + 1 = 0$.

Let $\gamma = \alpha + \alpha^{-1}$.
Note $\gamma^2 = \alpha^2 + 2 + \alpha^{-2} \Rightarrow \alpha^2+\alpha^{-2} =\gamma^2 - 2$.
Substitute: $\gamma + (\gamma^2 - 2) + 1 = 0 \Rightarrow \gamma^2 + \gamma - 1 = 0$
Solve the quadratic: $\gamma = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt 5}{2} \Rightarrow \alpha + \alpha^{-1} = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt 5}{2}$.

Note $\gamma = \alpha + \alpha^{-1} \Rightarrow \alpha \gamma = \alpha^2 + 1 \Rightarrow  \alpha^2 - \gamma \alpha + 1 = 0$.
Solve $\alpha^2 - \gamma \alpha + 1 = 0$: $\alpha = \frac{\gamma \pm \sqrt{\gamma^2 - 4}}{2}$.
Substitute $\gamma = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt 5}{2}$: $\alpha = \frac{\sqrt 5 - 1 \pm \sqrt{ -2 \sqrt 5 - 10}}{4}$, $\frac{-\sqrt 5 - 1 \pm \sqrt{ 2 \sqrt 5 - 10}}{4}$ as we need to show.

**Extract $cos \, 72^{\circ}$ and $sin \, 72^{\circ}$**
$\alpha = cos \, 72^{\circ} + i \, sin \, 72^{\circ}$
$\alpha + \alpha^{-1}$
$= cos \, 72^{\circ} + i \, sin \, 72^{\circ} + (cos \, 72^{\circ} + i \, sin \, 72^{\circ})^{-1}$
$= cos \, 72^{\circ} + i \, sin \, 72^{\circ} + cos \, -72^{\circ} + i \, sin \, -72^{\circ}$ using DeMoivre's Law
$= cos \, 72^{\circ} + i \, sin \, 72^{\circ} + cos \, 72^{\circ} - i \, sin \, 72^{\circ}$
$= 2 cos \, 72^{\circ}$
Recall that  $\alpha + \alpha^{-1} = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt 5}{2}$.
Thus, $2 \, cos \, 72^{\circ} = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt 5}{2} \Rightarrow cos \, 72^{\circ} = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt 5}{4} \Rightarrow sin \, 72^{\circ} = \frac{\sqrt{10 + 2 \sqrt 5}}{4}$ using Pythagorean identity.

Notes
- De Moivre's Law: $(cos \, \theta + i \, sin \, \theta)^n = cos (n \theta) + i \, sin (n \theta)$
- Pythagorean Identity: $sin^2 \, \theta + cos^2 \, \theta = 1$
- This problem is about how symmetry simplifies equations. You start with $\alpha^4+\alpha^3+\alpha^2+\alpha+1 = 0$ which looks like a hard 4th-degree equation but by using the symmetry $\alpha \leftrightarrow \alpha^{-1}$, we reduce it to the quadratic $\gamma^2 + \gamma - 1 = 0$.

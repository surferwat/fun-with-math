Show that the formula $\alpha = \frac{1}{10}[t_1 + (t_2t_1^8)/t_1^8+(t_3t_1^7)/t_1^7+...]$ of $\S22$, where $t_2t_1^8, t_3t_1^7$, ..., are known quantities, gives an $11th$ root of unity when $t_1$ is replaced by any $10$th root of $t_1^{10}$.

Changing $\alpha$ to $\alpha^2$ in the formula gives $\alpha^2 = \frac{1}{10}[\beta^{-1} t + (t_2t^8)/(\beta^{-1}t)^8 + (t_3t^7)/(\beta^{-1}t)^7 + ...]$. Thus changing $t$ to $\beta^{-1}t$ in the formula gives $\alpha^2$. Similary, changing $\alpha$ to $\alpha^{2^k}$ in the formula shows that this 11th root of unity is given by the formula when $t$ is replaced by $\beta^{-k}t$. Thus each 11th root $\alpha$, $\alpha^2$, $\alpha^3$, $\alpha^4$,... $\alpha^{10}$ can be obtained by using a suitable root $\beta^{-k}t$ of $x^{10} = t^{10}$ and these roots $\beta^{-k}t$ all give 11th roots of unity. 

Harold's argument shows that the number defined by the formula is an 11th root of unity.
Here is the logic cleanly:
- The formula produces some number $\alpha$.
- If you replace $t$ by another 10th root of $t^{10}$, namely $\beta^{-1}t$, the formula gives $\alpha^2$.
- Replacing $t$ by $\beta^{k}t$ gives $\alpha^{2^k}$. 
Now there are exactly $10$ different 10th roots of $t^{10}$,
So the formula produces exactly 10 numbers: $\alpha$, $\alpha^2$, $\alpha^{2^2}$, $\alpha^{2^3}$, ...
Because $2$ is a primitive root mod $11$, the powers $2^k (mod 11)$ run through $1,2,3,...,10$.
So the outputs are $\alpha$, $\alpha^2$, $\alpha^3$, ..., $\alpha^{10}$.
That means:
- These 10 numbers are all distinct.
- They are closed under multiplication.
- Multiplying exponents is happening modulo $11$.
The only way this can happen is if $\alpha^{11} = 1$.
So $\alpha$ is an 11th root of unity (in fact a primitive one, unless it accidentally equals $1$, which it doesn't in the construction).

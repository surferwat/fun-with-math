Show that the method of $\S6$ solves $x^4 + px^2 + qx + r=0$ algebraically in the sense that it writes the left side of this equation as a product of two factors of degree $2$ (which can then be written as products of linear factors using the quadratic formula).

$\mathit{Proof}$
WTS: $x^4 + px^2 + qx + r=(x^2+ax+b)(x^2-ax+c)$.
Recall Ferrari's idea and rewrite quartic as difference of squares:
$x^4 + px^2 + qx + r = (x^2+a)^2 - D(x-b)^2$
Using the identity $A^2-B^2 = (A+B)(A-B)$, we have:
$(x^2+a)^2 - D(x-b)^2=(x^2+a+\sqrt{D}(x-b))(x^2+a-\sqrt{D}(x-b))$.
So, $x^4 + px^2 + qx + r = (x^2+a+\sqrt{D}(x-b))(x^2+a-\sqrt{D}(x-b))$.

So that we can simplify the system, we attempt to express $D$ and $b$ in terms of $a$.
Expand:
$(x^2+a)^2 - D(x-b)^2$
$=x^4+2ax^2+a^2 -D(x^2-2xb+b^2)$
$=x^4 + (2a-D)x^2 +2bDx+(a^2-b^2D)$
Thus, we have:
$x^4 + px^2 + qx + r=x^4 + (2a-D)x^2 +2bDx+(a^2-b^2D)$
Match coefficients on right-hand side with those on left-hand side:
$p=2a-D$.
$q=2bD$.
$r=(a^2-b^2D)$.
Express in $D$ and $b$ in terms of $a$:
$D=-p+2a$.
$b=\frac{q}{2(-p+2a)}$.
Then substituting in $D$ and $b$, we have:
$(x^2+a+\sqrt{D}(x-b))(x^2+a-\sqrt{D}(x-b))$
$=(x^2+a+\sqrt{-p+2a}(x-\frac{q}{2(-p+2a)}))(x^2+a-\sqrt{-p+2a}(x-\frac{q}{2(-p+2a)}))$.
Thus, $x^4 + px^2 + qx + r=(x^2+a+\sqrt{-p+2a}(x-\frac{q}{2(-p+2a)}))(x^2+a-\sqrt{-p+2a}(x-\frac{q}{2(-p+2a)}))$, a product of two factors of degree $2$. $\blacksquare$

Notes
- **Demonstrates algebraic solvability of quartics:** It shows explicitly how any quartic polynomial can be factored into two quadratic polynomials, proving that quartic equations can be solved algebraically by radicals.
- **Illustrates Ferrari’s method concretely:** This exercise is a practical example of Ferrari’s classical approach to solving quartic equations, a landmark in algebra before the advent of Galois theory.
- **Links quartic solutions to cubic resolvent:** Finding the parameter $a$ requires solving a cubic equation, highlighting the deep connection between quartic equations and cubic resolvents in algebra.
- **Bridges abstract theory and explicit formulas:** It translates the theoretical notion that quartics are solvable into explicit factorization formulas, helping build intuition about the structure of polynomial roots. 
- **Foundation for modern algebra:** Understanding this factorization is key in appreciating the historical development of polynomial theory, Galois theory, and the classification of solvable equations.

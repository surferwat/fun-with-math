Prove that the Galois group of $x^4 + 1 = 0$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is the group presented in $\S42$.

Let $a$ be a root of $x^4 + 1 = 0$.
Then $a^4 = -1$, so $a^8 = 1$, and $a$ is a primitive $8$th root of unity. 
The other roots are $a,a^3,a^5,a^7$.
Hence the splitting field is $L = \mathbb{Q}(a)$.
Since $x^4+1 = \Phi_8(x)$, the eighth cyclotomic polynomial, it is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$. 
Therefore, $[L:\mathbb{Q}]=4$.
Because $L$ is the splitting field of a polynomial over a field of characteristic $0$, $| \operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})| =[L:\mathbb{Q}]=4$.
Now let $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})$. Since $\sigma$ fixes $\mathbb{Q}$, $0=\sigma(a^4+1)=\sigma(a)^4+1$, so $\sigma(a)$ must also be a root of $x^4 + 1$. Thus, $\sigma(a) \in \left\{a,a^3,a^5,a^7\right\}$.
Conversely, each of these four choices determines an automorphism of $L$, since $\mathbb{Q}(a)$ is generated by $a$ and each of $a,a^3,a^5,a^7$ satisfies the same equation $x^4+1=0$.
For such an automorphism, $\sigma(a^m) = \sigma(a)^m$, so the action on all four roots is obtained by multiplying exponents modulo $8$. 
The four automorphisms are therefore:

a a^3 a^5 a^7
a^3 a a^7 a^5
a^5 a^7 a a^3
a^7 a^5 a^3 a

which is exactly the group presented in $\S42$. Since the Galois group has order $4$, these are all of its elements.
Hence $\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})$ is precisely the four-element group presented $\S42$. 

Notes
- Since $a^4 = -1$, we have $a^8 = (a^4)^2 = (-1)^2 = 1$. Also, $a^4 \ne 1$, so the order of $a$ is not $4$. Thus the order of $a$ is $8$, meaning $a$ is a primitive 8th root of unity. The primitive $8$th roots of unity are precisely $a^k$ where $1 \le k \le 7$ and $gcd(k,8)=1$. The integers between $1$ and $7$ relatively prime to $8$ are $1,3,5,7$. Hence the primitive $8$th roots are $a,a^3,a^5,a^7$. These are exactly the roots of $x^4+1$, since $(a^k)^4 = a^{4k} = (a^4)^k = (-1)^k = -1$ for $k=1,3,5,7$ (all odd).
- If $L$ is the splitting field of a polynomial over a field $K$ of characteristic $0$, then the extension $L/K$ is Galois, because a splitting field is a normal extension and every polynomial over a field of characterstic $0$ is separable. A fundamental result of Galois theory then stats that the Galois group of the extension has exactly as many elements as the degree of the extension, so $|\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)| = [L:K]$.

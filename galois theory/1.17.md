Find an equation of degree $2$ whose roots are the cubes of the roots of $x^2+ax+b$. Apply this to the equation $x^2+x+1$ to conclude that its roots are complex cube roots of unity.

**Find the equation whose roots are $r_1^3$ and $r_2^3$.**
Let the roots of $x^2+ax+b$ be $r_1$ and $r_2$ then $r_1 + r_2 = -a$ and $r_1r_2=b$.
The new quadratic equations with roots $r_1^3$ and $r_2^3$ is $x^2-(r_1^3 + r_2^3)x+r_1^3r_2^3 = 0$.

Sum
Newton's Theorem gives us the symmetric polynomial $s_3 = r_1^3 + r_2^3 = s_2\sigma_1  - s_1\sigma_2 + 3\sigma_3$. 
Substituting $s_1=\sigma_1$ and $s_2=s_1\sigma_1-2\sigma_2$ recursively, we get $r_1^3 + r_2^3 = (r_1+r_2)^3 - 3r_1r_2(r_1+r_2) = (-a)^3 + 3ab$

Product
$r_1^3 r_2^3  = (r_1r_2)^3 = b^3$

**Construct the desired quadratic equation**
$x^2 - (r_1^3+r_2^3) + r_1^3r_2^3 = 0 \Rightarrow x^2 +(a^3 -3ab)x + b^3 = 0$

**Apply to $x^2+x+1=0$**
Here we have $a=1$ and $b=1$.
Substituting $a$ and $b$ into the quadratic equation that we found, we have $x^2 + (1^3-3(1)(1))x+1^3 = 0 \Rightarrow x^2-2x+1=0 \Rightarrow (x-1)^2=0$. 
Thus, $r_1^3 = r_2^3= 1$.

**Conclude roots are complex cube roots of unity**
The roots of $x^2+x+1=0$ satisfy $r^3 = 1$ but $r \ne 1$ since $1^2+1+1=3 \ne 0$.
Thus, they are exactly the non-real cube roots of unity: $\omega=e^{2 \pi i / 3}=-1/2+i\sqrt{3}/2$ and $\omega^2=e^{4 \pi i / 3}=-1/2-i\sqrt{3}/2$.

Notes
- This problem illustrates a core idea of Galois theory: symmetric functions of roots can be expressed using the coefficients, allowing us to build new polynomials (resolvents) whose roots are algebraic functions of the original ones.
- By constructing the quadratic whose roots are r13r13​ and r23r23​, we avoid solving the equation directly and instead reveal hidden structure.

Show that the six quantities $\pm(x-y+z-r)$, $\pm(x+y-z-r)$, $\pm(x-y-z+r)$ contain two, say $s$ and $t$, such that $s \ne \pm t$, unless at least three of $x,y,z,r$ are equal.

Let $A = x-y+z-r$, $B = x+y-z-r$, $C = x-y-z+r$. 

The six quantities in the problem are $\pm A, \pm B, \pm C$. 

We prove the contrapositive: If all six quantities are equal up to sign, then at least three of $x,y,z,r$ are equal.

Equivalently assume $|A| = |B| = |C|$.

**Step 1: Compare $A$ and $B$**
From $A^2 = B^2$, $(A-B)(A+B) = 0$.
Compute: $A-B = 2(z-y)$, $A+B = 2(x-r)$.
Hence $z=y$ or $x=r$. (1)

**Step 2: Compare $A$ and $C$**
From $A^2 = C^2$, $(A-C)(A+C) = 0$.
Compute: $A-C = 2(z-r)$, $A+C = 2(x-y)$.
Hence $z=r$ or $x=y$. (2)

**Step 3: Compare $B$ and $C$**
From $B^2 = C^2$, $(B-C)(B+C) = 0$.
Compute: $B - C = 2(y-r)$, $B+C = 2(x-z)$.
Hence: $y=r$ or $x=z$. (3)

**Step 4: Combine the possibilities**
From (1), (2), and (3) for each pair at least one equality must hold.
If none of $x=y$, $x=z$, $x=r$ holds, then (1)-(3) force $y=z$, $z=r$, $y=r$, so $y=z=r$: three variables are equal.
If one of $x=y$, $x=z$, $x=r$ holds, say $x=y$, then (2) and (3) force either $z=r$ or $x=z$; in either case, at least three of $x,y,z,r$ are equal.

In all cases, the assumption $|A| = |B| = |C|$ implies that at least three of $x,y,z,r$ are equal.

Conclusion
Therefore, unless at least three of $x,y,z,r$ are equal, the six quantities $\pm(x-y+z-r)$, $\pm(x+y-z-r)$, $\pm(x-y-z+r)$ cannot all be equal up to sign. Hence there exist two of them, say $s$ and $t$ s.t. $s \ne \pm t$. 

Notes
- At face value, the problem is about comparing linear expressions and their signs but the real object of study is how permutations of variables affect algebraic expressions, and how equality constraints force symmetry.

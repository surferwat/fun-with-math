Show that the Galois group acts transitively on the roots of a factor of $f$ if and only if the factor is irreducible over $K$. (To say that the group acts transitively on the roots means that given any two roots $a$ and $b$ there is a substitution of the group which carries $a$ to $b$.)

Let $g(x)$ be a factor of $f(x)$.
Let $L$ be the splitting field of $f$ over $K$.
Let $R$ be the set of roots of $g$ in $L$. 
Since every element of the Galois group fixes $K$, it carries roots of $g$ to roots of $g$. 
Thus the Galois group acts on $R$.

We shall show that this action is transitive if and only if $g$ is irreducible over $K$.

**$(\Leftarrow)$ Suppose first that $g$ is irreducible.**
Assume, to the contrary, that the action is not transitive.
Then the roots of $g$ split into at least two transitive classes under the action of the Galois group (i.e., if the action is not transitive, then there are roots of $g$ that cannot be reached from one another by substitutions in the Galois group).
Let $C$ be one such class, and define $h(x) = \prod_{a \in C} (x - a)$.
Every substitution in the Galois group merely permutes the roots in $C$, so the coefficients of $h$ are unchanged by every element of the Galois group. 
Therefore the coefficients of $h$ belong to $K$, and hence $h(x) \in K[x]$.
Since $C$ is a proper nonempty subset of the roots of $g$, the polynomial $h$ is a proper nonconstant factor of $g$. 
This contradicts the assumption that $g$ is irreducible.
Therefore, the action of the Galois group on the roots of $g$ must be transitive.

**$(\Rightarrow)$ Conversely, suppose the action is transitive.**
Assume that $g$ is reducible over $K$. 
Then $g = g_1 g_2$ with $g_1, g_2 \in K[x]$ nonconstant.
Let $\alpha$ be a root of $g_1$. 
For any substitution $\sigma$ in the Galois group, $g_1(\sigma(\alpha)) = \sigma(g_1(\alpha)) = \sigma(0) = 0$, since $\sigma$ fixes the coefficients of $g_1$. 
Thus $\sigma(\alpha)$ is again a root of $g_1$. 
Hence the set of roots of $g_1$ is preserved by every substitution of the Galois group.
In particular, if $\alpha$ is a root of $g_1$ and $\sigma$ is any substitution in the Galois group, then $\sigma(\alpha)$ is also a root of $g_1$. Therefore a root of $g_1$ can never be carried to a root of $g_2$ since every image of a root of $g_1$ is again a root of $g_1$. Hence no substitution can carry a root of $g_1$ outside the set of roots of $g_1$ and therefore it cannot reach all roots of $g$. 
Therefore the action on the roots of $g$ is not transitive, contrary to assumption.
Hence $g$ cannot be reducible. Therefore $g$ is irreducible.

We conclude that the Galois group acts transitively on the roots of a factor $g$ of $f$ if and only if $g$ is irreducible over $K$.

Notes
- This problem is significant because it shows that an irreducible polynomial has the property that its roots form a single orbit under the action of the Galois group, meaning that starting from any one root, every other root can be obtained by applying some substitution in the Galois group, so there is only one connected class of roots under these symmetries rather than several separate classes.
- $K[x]$ reads $K$ adjoin $x$ which means the set of all polynomials in the variable $x$ whose coefficients lie in the field $K$.

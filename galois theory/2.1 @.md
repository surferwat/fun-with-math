Show that the six permutations of $x$, $y$, and $z$ can be obtained as compositions of the cyclic permutation $x \rightarrow y \rightarrow z \rightarrow x$ and the interchange $y \rightarrow z$.

Let $c$ be the cyclic permutation $c : x \mapsto y$, $y \mapsto z$, $z \mapsto x$, $s$ be the interchange (transposition) $s : y \leftrightarrow z$, $x$ fixed. We want to show that every permutation of $\{x, y, z\}$ can be written as a composition of $c$ and $s$. 

**Step 1: List all six permutations of $x$,$y$,and $z$.** 
The symmetric group $S_3$ has six elements:
1. Identity: $(x,y,z)$
2. Cyclic permutations: $(x,y,z) \mapsto (y,z,x)$, $(x,y,z) \mapsto (z,x,y)$
3. Transpositions: $y \leftrightarrow z$, $x \leftrightarrow y$, $x \leftrightarrow z$

**Step 2: What permutations do we get from $c$?**
Compute powers of $c$: 
$c^0 = id$
$c^1 : (x,y,z) \mapsto (y,z,x)$
$c^2 : (x,y,z) \mapsto (z,x,y)$
$c^3 : id$
So $c$ gives all $3$-cycles.

**Step 3: What happens when we combine $c$ with $s$?**
We already have one transposition: $s = (y,z)$.
Now compute conjugates of $s$ by powers of $c$:
(a) $csc^{-1}$
Apply $c^{-1}$, then $s$, then $c$:  $csc^{-1} : x \leftrightarrow y$
So we get the transposition $(x,y)$.
(b) $c^2sc^{-2}$
Similarly: $c^2sc^{-2} :  x \leftrightarrow z$
So we get the transposition $(x,z)$.

**Step 4: Collect everything we can generate**
From $c$ and $s$, we have: 
Identity
$c, c^2$ (the two nontrivial 3-cycles)
$s = (y \, z)$
$csc^{-1} = (x \, y)$
$c^2sc^{-2} = (x \, z)$
That is all six permutations in $S_3$.

Thus, every permutation of $x,y,z$ can be written as a composition of:
- the cyclic permutation $x \mapsto y \mapsto z \mapsto x$ and
- the interchange $y \leftrightarrow z$.
Symbolically, $S_3 = \langle c, s \rangle$.

Notes
- The significance of this problem is conceptual, not computational. It explains why the method for solving the cubic works, and it introduces - quitely but powerfully - the core idea behind Galois theory.
- There is a fundamental principle "Any expression in the roots that is invariant under all permutations can be written using the coefficients of the polynomial". So this permutation problem is what justifies the satement: $u + v$ and $uv$ can be written in terms of the coefficients. Without this step, that claim would be hand-waiving.
- The cubic works because: (1) the permutation group of three objects $(S_3)$ is generated by simple symmetries; (2) these symmetries allow us to reduce the problem to a quadratic in $u$ and $v$; (3) quadratic equations are solvable by radicals. This group-structure is exactly what allows the method to succeed.
- Intuitive picture: think of three objects placed at the corners of a triangle: (1) the cyclic permutation is a rotation of the triangle; (2) the swap is a reflection. All symmetries of an equilateral triangle can be built from rotations and reflections - and those symmetries are exactly $S_3$.

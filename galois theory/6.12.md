Relate the solution of the cubic in Exercise 1 to the solution in $\S5$.

The key point is that the quantities $A$ and $B$ in §5 are exactly the quantities $U/3$ and $-U'/3$ that arise in Exercise 1. 
In §5, for the depressed cubic $x^3+px+q=0$, one introduces $a,b$ satisfying $3ab=p$ and $a^3-b^3+q=0$, and then $x=a-b$ is a root.
Eliminating $b$ gives $27A^6+27qA^3-p^3=0$ for $A=a$. 
In Exercise 1, we instead obtain $U^3=(-27q+3\sqrt{-3}\Delta)/2$ and $UU'=-3p$, where $\Delta^2=-4p^3-27q^2$. 
Set $A=U/3$ and $B=-U'/3$. 
Then $3AB=3(U/3)(-U'/3)=-UU'/3=p$, so the relation $3AB=p$ from §5 is exactly the relation $UU'=-3p$ from Exercise 1. 
Moreover, $A^3=(U/3)^3=(-27q+3\sqrt{-3}\Delta)/54$, which is one of the two roots of $27A^6+27qA^3-p^3=0$. 
Indeed, the quadratic formula gives $A^3=(-27q\pm3\sqrt{-3}\Delta)/54$, since $(-3)\Delta^2=12p^3+81q^2$. 
The other root is $B^3=-(U'/3)^3$. 
Thus the two methods are choosing the two cube-root quantities in the same quadratic resolvent, just with different notation. 
Finally, the §5 solution $A-B$ becomes $A-B=U/3-(-U'/3)=(U+U')/3$, which is precisely the solution obtained in Exercise 1. 
Multiplying $U$ and $U'$ by powers of $\alpha$ gives the other three pairings, so the three roots in Exercise 1 are exactly the three possible values of $A-B$ in the §5 method. 
Thus Exercise 1 and §5 are not two different solutions of the cubic: Exercise 1 is the explicit Galois-theoretic construction of the quantities $A$ and $B$ used in §5, with $A=U/3$ and $B=-U'/3$.

Author's solution
The solution of $\S5$ is $A-B$ where $A$ and $B$ are determined by $27A^6+27qA^3 - p^3 =0$, $3AB =p$. The solution of Exercise 1 is $(U+U')/3$ where $U$ is an explicit element of a field obtained by adjoining first a square root, then $\sqrt{-3}$, and then a cube root, and where $UU' = -3p$. In the new field, the quadratic equation satisfied by $A^3$ has two solutions, one of which is $(U/3)^3$. If $A=U/3$ then $B=-U'/3$ and the solutions coincide. If $A=\alpha^j U/3$, then the solutions coincide with one of the other solutions in Exercise 1. The product of the two solutions for $A^3$ is $(-p/3)^3 = (UU'/9)^3$ so the other solution is $(U'/3)^3$ and the three solutions are again by Exercise 1.

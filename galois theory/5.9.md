Show that the polynomial $F(X)$ of $\S38$ has the property that all of its irreducible factors have the same degree.

Let $t=Aa + Bb + Cc + ...$ .
By the definition of the resolvent, $F(X) = \prod_{S \in S_n} (X - S(t))$, where $S$ ranges over all permutations of the roots $a,b,c,...$ . 
Thus the roots of $F(X)$ are precisely the numbers $S(t)$. 
Now let $G(X)$ be any irreducible factor of $F(X)$, and let $u$ be a root of $G(X)$. Since $G(X) | F(X)$, $u$ is one of the roots of $F$, so $u = S(t)$ for some permutation $S$. 
Because $S$ merely permutes the roots $a,b,c,...$ it extends to a $K-$automorphism of the field $L = K(a,b,c,...)$. 
Since $L=K(t)$, we have $u = S(t) \in K(t)$, so $K(u) \subseteq K(t)$. 
Applying the inverse permutation $S^{-1}$, $t = S^{-1}(u) \in K(u)$, so $K(t) \subseteq K(u)$. 
Hence $K(u) = K(t)$.
Now the degree of the minimal polynomial of $u$ over $K$ is $[K(u) : K]$, and since $G(X)$ is the minimal polynomial of $u$ over $K$, $\deg G = [K(u) : K]$.
Because $K(u) = K(t)$ $\deg G = [K(t) : K]$. 
The right-hand side is independent of the choice of the irreducible factor $G$. 
Therefore every irreducible factor of $F(X)$ has the same degree.

Let $K$ be the field of quantities that are assumed to be known, and let $f(x)=0$ be a polynomial equation of degree $n$ with coefficients in $K$. Let $a,b,c,...$ be the roots of $f(x)=0$. (Galois and his predecessors, going all the way back to Girard 200 years earlier, seem to have taken it for granted that it was meaningful to talk about the roots of $f(x)$ and that there were precisely $n$ roots. They do not seem to have worried about where or what these roots were. This question will be addressed in $\S49$ et seq.) It will be assumed that the roots $a,b,c,...$ are distinct, a restriction which does not cost any generality because there is a simple technique for replacing for replacing an equation $f(x)=0$ with multiple roots with another equation with the same roots, all occuring once. Let $K(a,b,c)$ denote the field of all elements that can be expressed as rational functions of the roots $a,b,c,...$ Then there is an element $t \in K(a,b,c,...)$ called a Galois resolvent, with the property that any given element of $K(a,b,c,...)$ can be expressed as a polynomial in $t$ with coefficients in $K$. In fact, $t$ can be taken to have the form $t = Aa + Bb + Cc +...$ where $A,B,C,...$ are integers. Let $F(X)$ be the polynomial $F(X)= \prod(X-ASa-BSb-CSc-...)$ with coefficients in $K$, where the product is over all $n!$ permutations $S$ of the roots $a,b,c,...$ and let $F(X) = G_1(X)G_2(X)...G_k(X)$ be a decomposition of $F$ into irreducible factors. Then $F(t) =0$, and consequently, $t$ is a root of one of the polynomials $G_i$, say $G_1(t)=0$. Then the field $K(t)$, which is isomorphic to $K(a,b,c,...)$ can be represented very concretely as the simple algebraic extension of $K$ obtained by adjoining a root of the irreducible polynomial $G_1$.

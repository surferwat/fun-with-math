Find all quadratic equations $x^2 + ax +b = 0$ with the property that the squares of the roots coincide with the roots.

Let the roots be $r_1, r_2$.
Let $\{r_1^2, r_2^2\} = \{r_1, r_2\}$.

**Case 1 Each root is fixed by squaring**
$r^2=r$ for $r=0$ and $r=1$.
So, the possible root sets are as follows:
$\{0,0\} \Rightarrow x^2 = 0$.
$\{1,1\} \Rightarrow (x-1)^2=x^2-2x+1 = 0$.
$\{0,1\} \Rightarrow x(x-1)=x^2-x = 0$.

**Case 2 Squaring swaps the roots**
$r_1^2=r_2$, $r_2^2=r_1$.
$(r_1^2)^2 = (r_2)^2 = r_1$, so $r_1 = r_1^4 \Rightarrow r_1(r_1^3-1)=0$.
Excluding $r = 0, 1$ (already covered), we get the non-real cube roots of unity:
$r_1 = \omega$, $r_2 = \omega^2$, $\omega^3 = 1$, $\omega \ne 1$.
Sum
$r_1 + r_2 = \omega + \omega^2 = -1$ (since $1 + \omega + \omega^2 = 0$).
Product
$r_1r_2 = \omega\omega^2 = 1$
So, the equation is $x^2 - (-1)x + 1 = x^2 + x + 1 = 0$.

Therefore, all quadratic equations $x^2 + ax+b = 0$ are $x^2 = 0$, $x^2-x=0$, $x^2 - 2x + 1 = 0$, and $x^2 + x + 1 = 0$.

Notes:
- Shows how to think about **roots as a set**, not individually
- Uses **invariance under a function** (squaring) to restrict possibilities
- Introduces **fixed points and cycles** ($r2=r$ and root swapping)
- Naturally leads to **complex roots of unity**
- Demonstrates that **real-looking conditions can force complex solutions**
- Serves as a bridge to ideas in **symmetry, iteration, and higher algebra**

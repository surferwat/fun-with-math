Use Newton's Theorem to derive the formulas for "every r^i" $(i=1,2, ..., 8)$ in $\S9$.

Newton's Theorem is the relation $s_k -s_{k-1}\sigma_1 + s_{k-2}\sigma_2 - ... + (-1)^{k-1}s_1\sigma_{k-1}+(-1)^kk\sigma_k=0$ for $(k=1,2,3,...)$ where, as is natural, $\sigma_j=0$ for $j > n$ and where $s_k$ is the symmetric polynomial for $k=1,2,3$, $s_k=r_1^k+r_2^k+...+r_n^k$.

every $r$
$k=1$
Theorem gives us $s_1-s_0\sigma_1$ where $s_1=r_1^1+r_2^1+...+r_n^1$ and $s_0=1$.
$s_1-s_0\sigma_1=0$
$s_1=s_0\sigma_1$
$s_1=\sigma_1$

every $r^2$
$k=2$
Theorem gives us $s_2 - s_1\sigma_1+s_0\sigma_2=0$ where $s_2 = r_1^2 + r_2^2 + ... + r_n^2$ and $s_1= r_1+r_2+...+r_n$
$s_2 - s_1\sigma_1+s_0\sigma_2=0$
$s_2 = s_1\sigma_1-s_0\sigma_2$
$s_2=\sigma_1^2-2\sigma_2$

Notes
- This exercise illustrates—and strongly reinforces—the **Fundamental Theorem of Symmetric Polynomials**:  any symmetric expression in the roots of a polynomial can be written entirely in terms of the coefficients of the polynomial, without ever knowing the roots themselves. Newton’s identities give this explicitly. By expressing the power sums $s_k=r_1^k+r_2^k+ ... +r_n^k$ in terms of the elementary symmetric polynomials $\sigma_1$, $\sigma_2$​,…, we show that information about the roots can be **recovered algebraically** from the coefficients alone. This allows for the computing of sums of powers of eigenvalues, computing traces of matrix powers, computing moments of distributions related to roots, analyzing stability of differential equations.
- Symmetric functions in the roots are central in Galois theory.  Newton’s identities guarantee that _every symmetric function of the roots_ can be written in terms of the coefficients. This is the fundamental reason why the Galois group permutes the roots but leaves the coefficients fixed.

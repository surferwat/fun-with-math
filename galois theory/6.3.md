Let $a,b,c,d$ be the roots of a quartic equation and let $P=ab+cd, Q=ac+bd, R=ad+bc$. Show that the discriminant of the cubic of which $P,Q,R$ are the roots is equal to the discriminant of the quartic of which $a,b,c,d$ are the roots. Use this fact to find the discriminant of the quartic. Assume that $a+b+c+d=0$ to simplify the computations. Describe how the formula in this special case can be used to find the general formula without carrying this out.

Let $f(x)=x^4-\sigma_1x^3+\sigma_2x^2-\sigma_3x+\sigma_4$ have roots $a,b,c,d$, and define $P=ab+cd$, $Q=ac+bd$, and $R=ad+bc$. 
Assume throughout that $a+b+c+d=0$, so that $\sigma_1=0$. 
This is equivalent to writing the quartic in the depressed form $f(x)=x^4+\alpha x^2+\beta x+\gamma$, where $\alpha=\sigma_2$, $\beta=-\sigma_3$, and $\gamma=\sigma_4$.

Step 1. Equality of the discriminants
To compare the discriminants, we compute the pairwise differences $P-Q$, $Q-R$, and $R-P$, since the discriminant of the cubic with roots $P,Q,R$ is $(P-Q)^2(Q-R)^2(R-P)^2$.
First observe that $P-Q=(ab+cd)-(ac+bd)=a(b-c)-d(b-c)=(a-d)(b-c).$
Similarly, $Q-R=(a-b)(c-d)$ and $R-P=(a-c)(d-b)=-(a-c)(b-d)$. 
Therefore, $(P-Q)(Q-R)(R-P)=\pm(a-b)(a-c)(a-d)(b-c)(b-d)(c-d),$ where the sign is immaterial after squaring. 
Hence $(P-Q)^2(Q-R)^2(R-P)^2=\prod_{i<j}(r_i-r_j)^2.$
The left-hand side is the discriminant of the cubic with roots $P,Q,R$, while the right-hand side is the discriminant of the quartic with roots $a,b,c,d$. 
Thus $\boxed{\Delta_{\text{quartic}}=\Delta_{\text{cubic}}}.$

Step 2. The resolvent cubic
The cubic having roots $P,Q,R$ is $(x-P)(x-Q)(x-R)=x^3-(P+Q+R)x^2+(PQ+QR+RP)x-PQR$.
From the identities established in the preceding exercise,
$P+Q+R=\sigma_2, PQ+QR+RP=\sigma_1\sigma_3-4\sigma_4,PQR=\sigma_1^2\sigma_4+\sigma_3^2-4\sigma_2\sigma_4.$
Since $\sigma_1=0$, we obtain $g(x)=x^3-\sigma_2x^2-4\sigma_4x-(\sigma_3^2-4\sigma_2\sigma_4).$
Substituting $\sigma_2=\alpha$, $\sigma_3=-\beta$, and $\sigma_4=\gamma$ gives $g(x)=x^3-\alpha x^2-4\gamma x-(\beta^2-4\alpha\gamma).$

Step 3. Compute the discriminant
For a cubic $x^3+Ax^2+Bx+C$, the discriminant is $\Delta=A^2B^2-4B^3-4A^3C-27C^2+18ABC.$
Here $A=-\alpha$, $B=-4\gamma$, and $C=-(\beta^2-4\alpha\gamma)$. 
Substituting and simplifying yields $\boxed{\Delta=-128\alpha^2\gamma^2+16\alpha^4\gamma-4\alpha^3\beta^2+144\alpha\beta^2\gamma+256\gamma^3-27\beta^4.}$
Since the quartic and the resolvent cubic have the same discriminant, this is also the discriminant of the depressed quartic $x^4+\alpha x^2+\beta x+\gamma$.

General quartic
For a general quartic $x^4+bx^3+cx^2+dx+e$, make the translation $x=y-\frac{b}{4}$ in order to reduce the quartic to the case $\sigma_1=0$. 
This removes the cubic term without changing the differences of the roots, so the discriminant is unchanged. 
We may therefore apply the formula derived above for the depressed quartic. 
The translated polynomial has the form $y^4+\alpha y^2+\beta y+\gamma$, whose discriminant is $\Delta=-128\alpha^2\gamma^2+16\alpha^4\gamma-4\alpha^3\beta^2+144\alpha\beta^2\gamma+256\gamma^3-27\beta^4.$ 
Expressing $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ in terms of $b,c,d,e$ then yields the discriminant formula for an arbitrary quartic.

Author's solution
$P-Q=(a-d)(b-c)$. Thus $(P-Q)(Q-R)(R-P) = \pm(a-b)(a-c)(a-d)(b-c)(b-d)(c-d)=\pm \text{Vandermonde determinant of } a,b,c,d$. The discriminant of the quartic is therefore equal to the discriminant of the cubic $X^3-(P+Q+R)X^2 + (PQ+QR+RP)X-PQR=0$. By the formulas of the preceding exercise, this is the cubic $x^3-\sigma_2 x^2 + (\sigma_1 \sigma_3 - 4 \sigma_4)x - (\sigma_1^2 \sigma_4 + \sigma_3^2 - 4 \sigma_2 \sigma_4$). If the quartic has the form $x^4+\alpha x^2 + \beta x + \gamma$ where $\sigma_1 = 0$ then by the formula of $\S13$, this discriminant is $-128\alpha^2 \gamma^2 + 16 \alpha^4 \gamma -- 4 \alpha^3 \beta^2 + 144 \alpha \beta^2 \gamma + 256 \gamma^3 - 27 \beta^4$.

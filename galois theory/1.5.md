Find the complex roots of the equation of Exercise 3 by: (a) solving the quadratic equation they satisfy; and (b) using complex values of $a$ in the solution of the cubic. 

Answer for (a) 
WTS: $x=-1\pm3i$.
Let $x^2+2x+10$ be the quadratic from Exercise 3.
Let $a=1$, $b=2$, and $c=10$.
Using the quadratic formula, we get:
$x=\frac{-2\pm\sqrt{4-40}}{2}=\frac{-2\pm\sqrt{-36}}{2}=\frac{-2\pm6i}{2}=-1\pm3i$.

Answer for (b)
WTS: $x=-1\pm3i$.
Let $x^3+6x=20$ be the cubic to be solved from Exercise 3.
Let $x=\sqrt[3]{10 + \sqrt{108}}-\sqrt[3]{-10+ \sqrt{108}}$ be a real solution to the cubic.
Let $\omega = \frac{1}{2}[-1 + i \sqrt{3})]$ be a complex solution to $\omega^3=1$.
Then from Exercise 4 we know that:
$x=\omega\sqrt[3]{10 + \sqrt{108}}-\omega^2\sqrt[3]{-10+ \sqrt{108}}$.
$x=\omega^2\sqrt[3]{10 + \sqrt{108}}-\omega\sqrt[3]{-10+ \sqrt{108}}$.
And from Exercise 3 we know that:
$A=\sqrt[3]{10 + \sqrt{108}}=\sqrt[3]{(1+\sqrt{3})^3}=1+\sqrt{3}$.
$B=\sqrt[3]{-10 + \sqrt{108}}=\sqrt[3]{(-1+\sqrt{3})^3}=-1+\sqrt{3}$

We substitute and solve for $x$:
$x=(-\frac{1}{2} + \frac{i \sqrt{3})}{2}) A-(-\frac{1}{2} + \frac{i \sqrt{3})}{2})^2 B$.
$x=(-\frac{1}{2} + \frac{i \sqrt{3})}{2}) A-(-\frac{1}{2} - \frac{i \sqrt{3})}{2}) B$.
$x=-\frac{1}{2}A+\frac{1}{2}B + \frac{i \sqrt{3}}{2}A+\frac{i \sqrt{3}}{2}B$.
$x=-\frac{1}{2}(A-B) + \frac{i \sqrt{3}}{2}(A+B)$.
$x=-\frac{1}{2}[(1+\sqrt{3})-(-1+\sqrt{3})) + \frac{i \sqrt{3}}{2}[(1+\sqrt{3})+(-1+\sqrt{3})]$.
$x=-\frac{1}{2}2 + \frac{i \sqrt{3}}{2}2\sqrt{3}$.
$x=-1 + 3i$.

We substitute and solve for $x$:
$x=(-\frac{1}{2} + \frac{i \sqrt{3})}{2})^2 A-(-\frac{1}{2} + \frac{i \sqrt{3})}{2}) B$.
$x=(-\frac{1}{2} + \frac{i \sqrt{3})}{2})^2 A-(-\frac{1}{2} + \frac{i \sqrt{3})}{2}) B$.
$x=(-\frac{1}{2} - \frac{i \sqrt{3})}{2}) A-(-\frac{1}{2} + \frac{i \sqrt{3})}{2}) B$.
$x=-\frac{1}{2}A+\frac{1}{2}B - \frac{i \sqrt{3}}{2}A-\frac{i \sqrt{3}}{2}B$.
$x=-\frac{1}{2}(A-B) - \frac{i \sqrt{3}}{2}(A+B)$.
$x=-\frac{1}{2}[(1+\sqrt{3})-(-1+\sqrt{3})] - \frac{i \sqrt{3}}{2}[(1+\sqrt{3})+(-1+\sqrt{3})]$.
$x=-\frac{1}{2}2 - \frac{i \sqrt{3}}{2}2\sqrt{3}$.
$x=-1 - 3i$.

Thus, the complex roots of $x^3+6x=20$ are $x=-1\pm3i$ as expected.

Notes
- This exercise highlights a major historical and conceptual shift in mathematics - even though the original equation is real and has real coefficients, complex numbers must be used in intermediate steps (especially in Cardano's method), even if the final roots are real or complex conjugates.
- This exercise offers two perspectives - one via quadratic roots and one via algebraic manipulation and complex roots of unity - that arrive at the same truth.

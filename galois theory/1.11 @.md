Given a polynomial in $n$ variables $f(x_1,x_2, ..., x_n)$, let its terms be ordered as follows. Arrange terms first according to the power of $x_1$ they contain: $f = A_h x_1^h+A_{h-1}x_1^{h-1}+ ... + A_1x_1 + A_0$ where the $A$'s are polynomials in $x_2, x_3, ... , x_n$. Next arrange the terms of $A_i$ according to the powers of $x_2$ they contain: $A_i = B_{ik}x_2^k + ... + B_{i1}x_2 + B_{i0}$, then arrange the terms of $B_{ij}$ according to the powers of $x_3$ they contain, etc. Otherwise stated, put the terms of $f$ in lexicographic order, where one term precedes another in lexicographic order if it contains $x_1$ to a higher power, or, if they contain $x_1$ and $x_2$ to the same power, it contains $x_3$ to a higher power, etc. Show that if $f$ and $g$ are two polynomials in $x_1, x_2, ..., x_n$ in lexicographic order then the leading term of $fg$ is equal to the leading term of $f$ times the leading term of $g$.  

$\mathit{Proof}$
Let $f=\sum c_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}$ s.t. $\alpha \in \mathbb{N}$ and $x^{\alpha} = x_1^{\alpha_1}x_2^{\alpha_2}...x_n^{\alpha_n}$.
Let $g=\sum d_{\beta}x^{\beta}$ s.t. $\beta \in \mathbb{N}$ and $x^{\beta} = x_1^{\beta_1}x_2^{\beta_2}...x_n^{\beta_n}$.
Let the leading term of $f$ in lexicographical order be $LT(f)=c_{\alpha^*}x^{\alpha^*}$. 
Let the leading term of $g$  in lexicographical order be $LT(g)=d_{\beta^*}x^{\beta^*}$. 
WTS: $LT(fg) = LT(f)LT(g) = c_{\alpha^*}d_{\beta^*}x^{\alpha^*+\beta^*}$.
$fg = (\sum c_{\alpha}x^{\alpha})(\sum d_{\beta}x^{\beta}) = \sum c_{\alpha}d_{\beta}x^{\alpha+\beta}$
By assumption, $\alpha* > \alpha$ for all $\alpha \ne \alpha^*$ and $\beta^* > \beta$ for all  $\beta \ne \beta^*$.
Since lexicographical order is compitable addition, we have: $\alpha^*+\beta^* > \alpha+\beta$ .
Therefore, $x^{\alpha^*+\beta^*} > x^{\alpha+\beta}$ under lexicographical order.
This means that $x^{\alpha^*+\beta^*}$ is the largest term with $c_{\alpha^*}d_{\beta^*}$ its coefficient.
Thus, $LT(fg) = LT(\sum c_{\alpha}d_{\beta}x^{\alpha+\beta}) = c_{\alpha^*}d_{\beta^*}x^{\alpha^*+\beta^*}= LT(f)LT(g)$. $\blacksquare$

Notes
- The main context for this result is in the theory of Gröbner bases, a powerful tool in computational algebra for solving systems of polynomial equations.

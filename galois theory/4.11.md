Combine Exercises 8 and 9 to express $a,b,c$ the roots of an irreducible cubic $x^3 + px + q = 0$, as polynomials in $t=a-b$.

The key conceptual structure is clear:
1. Exercise 9 gives the roots as rational functions of $t=a-b$.
2. Exercise 8 gives an algebraic relation satisfied by $t$.
3. That relation allows the denominator $t^2+p$ to be rewritten polynomially.
4. Therefore the roots become actual polynomials in $t$.

Let $f(x) = x^3 +px+q$ be an irreducible cubic with roots $a,b,c$ and let $t=a-b$.
Exercise 9 shows that the roots can be expressed rationally in terms of $t$.
We now combine this with the equation for $t$ from Exercise 8 to obtain polynomial expressions.

**Express $a$ and $b$ in terms of $c$ and $t$**
Since the coefficient of $x^2$ is $0$, $a+b+c=0$, so $a+b=-c$.
Together with $a-b=t$, this gives $a=\frac{-c+t}{2}$, $b=\frac{-c-t}{2}$ $(1)$.

**Express $a$ and $b$ rationally in terms of $t$**
Using $ab+ac+bc=p$, we compute $ab=\frac{(-c+t)(-c-t)}{4} = \frac{c^2-t^2}{4}$, while $ac+bc=c(a+b)=-c^2$.
Hence $p=\frac{c^2-t^2}{4}-c^2$, so $3c^2+t^2=-4p$ $(2)$.
Now use $abc = -q$.
Since $ab=\frac{c^2-t^2}{4}$, we obtain $c(c^2-t^2)=-4q$.
Substituting (2), $c^2=-\frac{t^2+4p}{3}$, gives $c(-\frac{t^2+4p}{3}-t^2)=-4q$.
Simplifying, $c(-\frac{4(t^2+p)}{3})=-4q$, and therefore $c=\frac{3q}{t^2+p}$ $(3)$.
Substituting into (1), $a=\frac{1}{2}(t-\frac{3q}{t^2+p})$, $b=-\frac{1}{2}(t+\frac{3q}{t^2+p})$.
So far the roots are rational functions of $t$.

**Use the sextic relation satisfied by $t$**
Now use Exercise 8, which showed that $t$ satisfies $(t^2+4p)(t^2+p)^2 + 27q^2 = 0$.
Rearranging, $(t^2+p)^2(t^2+4p) = -27q^2$.
Since $q \ne 0$, $\frac{1}{t^2+p} = - \frac{(t^2+p)(t^2+4p)}{27q^2}$ $(4)$.

**Eliminate denominators to obtain polynomial expressions**
Substituting $(4)$ into $(3)$, $c=\frac{3q}{t^2+p}=-\frac{(t^2+p)(t^2+4p)}{9q}$.
Expanding, $c=-\frac{t^4+5pt^2+4p^2}{9q}$.
Finally, $a=\frac{-c+t}{2}$, $b=\frac{-c-t}{2}$, so $a=\frac{t^4+5pt^2+4p^2+9qt}{18q}$ and $b=\frac{t^4+5pt^2+4p^2-9qt}{18q}$.

**Conclusion**
Thus $a,b,c$ are all polynomials in $t=a-b$.

Notes
- The significance of this problem is about how a splitting field can be generated by one carefully chosen element and how Galois symmetry becomes encoded algebraically. In other words, it shows how the entire splitting field of an irreducible cubic can be generated from a single non-symmetric root difference $t=a-b$ and how the permutations of roots become encoded in the algebraic equation satisfied by $t$.

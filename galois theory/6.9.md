Prove Galois' Proposition IV: if $u$ is in $K(a,b,c, \dots)$ - that is, if $u$ is a polynomial in the roots $a,b,c,...$ of $f(x)=0$ - then the Galois group of $f(x)=0$ over $K(u)$ is equal to the subgroup of the Galois group of $f(x)=0$ over $K$ consisting of those substitutions which leave $u$ fixed.

Every substitution of the group over $K(u)$ fixes every element of $K(u)$, and hence fixes every element of $K$, since $K\subseteq K(u)$. 
Therefore every substitution of the group over $K(u)$ is a substitution of the group over $K$. 
Also, since $u\in K(u)$, every substitution of the group over $K(u)$ leaves $u$ fixed by Proposition I.
Thus the group over $K(u)$ is contained in the subgroup of the group over $K$ consisting of those substitutions which leave $u$ fixed.

Conversely, let $S$ be a substitution of the group over $K$ such that $Su=u$. 
Let $H(X)$ be a polynomial in $X$ with coefficients in $K(u)$, and suppose that $t$ is a root of $H(X)=0$. 
Since $S$ leaves $K$ fixed and leaves $u$ fixed, it leaves every element of $K(u)$ fixed. 
Hence application of $S$ to $H(t)=0$ gives $H(St)=0$. 
Thus $S$ carries roots of every polynomial with coefficients in $K(u)$ to roots of the same polynomial. 
In particular, if $t=\phi_a(t),\phi_b(t),\phi_c(t),\ldots$ are the roots of $f(x)=0$, then $S$ carries these roots to $\phi_a(St),\phi_b(St),\phi_c(St),\ldots$. 
Since $H(St)=0$ whenever $H(t)=0$ for a polynomial $H(X)$ with coefficients in $K(u)$, this action of $S$ preserves every polynomial equation over $K(u)$. 
Hence $S$ is a substitution of the group over $K(u)$. 
Thus every substitution of the group over $K$ which leaves $u$ fixed belongs to the group over $K(u)$. 
The two groups are therefore equal.

Author's solution
Any substitution of the group over $K(u)$ is obviously in the group over $K$, and it leaves $u$ fixed by Proposition I. Conversely, if $S$ is a substitution in the group over $K$, if $Su=u$, and if $H(X)$ is a polynomial in $X$ with coefficients in $K(u)$ of which $t$ is a root then application of $S$ to $H(t)=0$ gives $H(St)=0$. Thus the group over $K(u)$ contains the substitution which carries $\phi_a(t), \phi_b(t), \phi_c(t), \dots$ to $\phi_a(St), \phi_b(St), \phi_c(St), \dots$ and this is the substitution $S$.

Gauss's method of $\S25$ applied to the case $p=7$, $D=6$, $d=2$ gives an algebraic expression for the solution $\gamma = \alpha + \alpha^{-1}$ of the equation $\gamma^3 + \gamma^2 -2\gamma -1 = 0$ of the beginning of $\S22$. Find this expression (which involves the cube root of unity $\omega$).

Let $p=7$ and let $\alpha$ be a primitive $7$th root of unity: $\alpha = e^{2 \pi i/7}$, $\alpha^7 = 1$, $\alpha \ne 1$.
Set $\gamma = \alpha + \alpha^{-1}$.
It is known (and stated in $\S22$) that $\gamma$ satisfies the cubic $\gamma^3 + \gamma^2 - 2\gamma - 1 = 0$.
We apply Gauss's method (as in $\S25$) with $p=7$, $D=6$, $d=2$.

**Step 1: Periods of length $d=2$**
The multiplicative group ($\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z})^x$ has order $6$.
A primitive root mod 7 is $g=3$ since $3^0 = 1$, $3^1 = 3$, $3^2 = 2$, $3^3 = 6$, $3^4 = 4$, $3^5 = 5$ (mod $7$) which produce all nonzero residues modulo 7.
Since there are $6$ nontrivial $7$th roots of unity and we take $d=2$, we group the roots into pairs. 
Using the primitive root $g=3$, the nonzero residues modulo $7$ appear in the order $1,3,2,6,4,5$. 
Grouping the roots into complex-conjugate pairs ($\alpha^k$, $\alpha^{7-k}$) produces the Gaussian periods: $(1,6), (3,4), (2,5)$ which correspond to the real sums $\eta_0 = \alpha + \alpha^6$, $\eta_1 = \alpha^3 + \alpha^4$, $\eta_2 = \alpha^2 + \alpha^5$. Each pair $(k,7-k)$ sums a root with its complex conjugate, ensuring the Gaussian periods are real.
In particular, $\eta_0 = \alpha + \alpha^{6} = \alpha + \alpha^{-1} = \gamma$. Hence $\gamma = \eta_0$ is one of the Gaussian periods while the other two periods $\eta_1$, $\eta_2$ give the remaining roots of the cubic $\eta^3 + \eta^2 - 2 \eta - 1 = 0$. 

**Step 2: Relations between the periods**
First, $\eta_0 + \eta_1 + \eta_2 = (\alpha + \alpha^2 + \alpha^3 + \alpha^4 + \alpha^5 + \alpha^6)$.
Since $1 + \alpha + \alpha^2 + ... + \alpha^6 = 0$, we obtain $\eta_0 + \eta_1 + \eta_2 = -1$.

**Step 3: Expressing $\eta_1$, $\eta_2$ via cube roots of unity**
Let $\omega = e^{2 \pi i/3}$ be a cube root of unity.
Gauss's periods reduce the $6$th-degree cyclotomic polynomial to a cubic for $\eta_0$, $\eta_1$, $\eta_2$. Since $\gamma = \eta_0$, an explicit algebraic expression for $\gamma$ in terms of cube roots is $\gamma = -\frac{1}{3} +\frac{1}{3}(\omega \sqrt[3]\frac{-7+21\sqrt{-3}}{2} + \omega^2 \sqrt[3]\frac{-7-21\sqrt{-3}}{2})$, where $\omega = e^{2 \pi i / 3}$. 
Although it involves complex cube roots, the sum is real, giving $\gamma = 2 cos(2\pi/7)$. 

**Step 4: Final expression**
Thus the solution $\gamma = \alpha + \alpha^{-1}$ of $\gamma^3 + \gamma^2 - 2\gamma -1  = 0$ is $\gamma = -\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3}(\omega \sqrt[3]{\frac{-7 + 21 \sqrt{-3}}{2}} + \omega^2 \sqrt[3]{\frac{-7 - 21 \sqrt{-3} }{2}})$
where $\omega = e^{2 \pi i / 3}$, $\omega^3 = 1$, $\omega \ne 1$.
The other two Gaussian periods $\eta_1$ and $\eta_2$ give the remaining roots of the cubic.

Notes
- This formula arises from Gaussian periods for $p=7$. It expresses the real quantity $\gamma = 2 cos (\frac{2 \pi}{7})$ in radicals using cube roots and cube roots of unity, exactly as predicted by Gauss's construction.
- This problem illustrates several deep ideas connecting classical algebra, cyclotomic fields, and Galois theory. Although it looks like a routine radical computation, it actually reflects a major historical development in mathematics.
	- It shows how roots of unity lead to solvable equations. The quantity $\gamma = \alpha + \alpha^{-1}$ comes from a primitive $7$th root of unity. The full cyclotomic equation for $7$ is $x^6 + x^5 + x^4 + x^3 + x^2 + x + 1 = 0$. By grouping conjugates ($\alpha + \alpha^{-1}$), we reduce the degree from $6$ to $3$. The resulting cubic $\gamma^3 + \gamma^2 - 2 \gamma - 1 = 0$ is the minimal polynomial of $2 cos(2\pi / 7)$. So the problem shows how complicated roots of unity can produce a simpler real algebraic numbers.
	- This method used here is Gaussian periods, introduced by Gauss. Gaussian periods systematically group roots of unity to produce numbers with smaller Galois orbits. In this case: the $6$ roots of the cyclotomic polynomial are grouped into 3 pairs, each pair gives a real quantity like $\alpha + \alpha^{-1}$. This technique was revolutionary because it allowed Gauss to explicitly compute algebraic expressions for the these numbers.
- It shows a typical phenomenon in algebra. The formula you obtain: involves complex cube roots but the final number is real. This phenomenon - complex radicals producing real numbers - is very common when solving equations algebraically.
- Gauss's technique as presented in $\S25$ for the solution of the problem was to construct a sequence of intermediate fields between the know field $\mathbb{Q}$ and the unknown field $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ and to regard the whole extension from $\mathbb{Q}$ to $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ as being made up of a sequence of extensions in which one ascends the ladder of intermediate extensions.

Derive Gauss's algebraic expression for $cos(2 \pi/17)$ in Section 365 of Disquisitiones Arithmeticae: $cos(2 \pi/17) = -\frac{1}{16} + \frac{1}{16} \sqrt 17 + \frac{1}{16}\sqrt{34-2\sqrt 17} + \frac{1}{8}\sqrt{17 + 3 \sqrt{17} - \sqrt{34-2\sqrt{17}} - 2 \sqrt{34 + 2\sqrt{17}}}$ 
(Evaluate numerically the elements of the fields $K_1, K_2, K_4$ in succession and use this to evaluate the needed cosine $(\alpha + \alpha^{-1})/2$ which is $K_8$. The main technique is the resolvent method described in $\S25$. However, the problem is not entirely algebraic because the signs of the radicals in Gauss's formula must be determined by non-algebraic means.)

Let $\zeta = e^{2 \pi i /17}$.
Then: $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)/\mathbb{Q}$ is a cyclotomic extension of degree $[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta) : \mathbb{Q}] = \phi(17) = 16$.
Its Galois group is $Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong(\mathbb{Z}/17\mathbb{Z})^x$, which is cyclic order $16$.
The identity $e^{i \theta} + e^{-i \theta} = 2 cos \theta$ gives us $\zeta + \zeta^{-1} = 2 cos (2 \pi / 17)$. 
So: $\mathbb{Q}( \zeta + \zeta^{-1}) = \mathbb{Q}(2cos (2 \pi / 17)) = \mathbb{Q}(cos (2 \pi / 17))$.
This is the maximal real subfield and it has a degree: $[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta + \zeta^{-1}) : \mathbb{Q}] = 8$.
So: $K_8 = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta + \zeta^{-1})$.
Since the Galois group is cyclic of order 16, it has unique subgroups of orders: $1,2,4,8,16$.
By Galois correspondence, this gives a tower of fields: $\mathbb{Q} = K_1 \subset K_2 \subset K_4 \subset K_8 \subset K_{16} = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$ where degree $[K_n : \mathbb{Q}] = n$ with each extension having degree $2$: $[K_{2^k} : K_{2^{k-1}}] = 2$.
Since $K_8/\mathbb{Q}$ is obtained by a sequence of quadratic extensions, $K_{i+1} = K_i(\sqrt{a_i})$ with $a_i \in K_i$, every element of $K_8$ can be expressed using rational operations and iterated square roots. In particular, $cos(2 \pi/17)$ admits an expression in nested square roots. This reflects the fact that the regular $17-$gon is constructible since its coordinates lie in a tower of quadratic extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Step 1: Choose a generator**
Let $\zeta = e^{2 \pi i/17}$ so $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Q}$ is a cyclotomic extension.
Then every automorphism in $Gal(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Q})$ is determined by $\sigma_a(\zeta) = \zeta^a$ for $a \in (\mathbb{Z}/17\mathbb{Z})^x$.
Hence, $Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/17 \mathbb{Z})^x$, which is a cyclic group of order $16$.
Since $(\mathbb{Z}/17\mathbb{Z})^x$ is cyclic, it has a primitive root; for example, $3 \in (\mathbb{Z}/17\mathbb{Z})^x$ generates the group.
Therefore, the corresponding automorphism $\sigma(\zeta) = \zeta^3$ generates $Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)/\mathbb{Q})$.
Let the chosen generator be $\sigma : \zeta \mapsto \zeta^3$.

**Step 2: Construct $K_2$ (quadratic split)**
 Let $\eta = \sum_{k \in QR} \zeta^k$, $\eta ' = \sum_{k \in NQR} \zeta^k$ where $QR$ and $NQR$ denote the quadratic residues and non-residues modulo $17$.
 Since these sets partition $(\mathbb{Z}/17\mathbb{Z}^{\times})$, we have $\zeta + \zeta' = \sum_{k=1}^{16} \zeta^k$.
 
 *Define sum*
 Using the identity $1 + \zeta + \zeta^2 + ... + \zeta^{16} = 0$, it follows that $\eta + \eta' = -1$.
 
 *Define product*
 Moreover, the product $\eta \eta'$ can be computed using Gaussian period multiplication.
 $\eta \eta' = \sum_{k \in QR} \sum_{k \in NQR} \zeta^{a+b}$. So we are summing $\zeta^{a+b}$ over all pairs $(a,b)$ with: $a$ a quadratic residue mod $17$ and $b$ a non-residue.
 In $(\mathbb{Z}/17\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$: there are 8 quadratic residues and 8 non-residues so total number of pairs $(a,b)$ is $8 \cdot 8 = 64$.
 What values does $a+b$ take? Fix a nonzero $k \in \{1,...,16 \}$. How many pairs $(a,b)$ satisfy $a \in QR, b \in NQR$ and $a+b \cong k \, mod \, 17$? Since multiplication by quadratic residue permutes both: the QR set and the NQR set, every nonzero residue $k$ occurs the same number of times as a sum $a+b$. So each $k \in \{1, ..., 16 \}$ appears equally often. 
 Since there are: $16$ possible nonzero residues and 64 total pairs, each residue appears $64/16 = 4$ times.
 So we can rewrite the sum $\eta \eta' = \sum_{k =1}^{16} 4\zeta^k = 4\sum_{k =1}^{16}\zeta^k$. 
 Now use the cyclotomic identity $1 + \zeta + \zeta^2 + ... + \zeta^{16} = 0$ so $\sum_{k =1}^{16}\zeta^k = -1 \Rightarrow \eta \eta' = 4\sum_{k =1}^{16}\zeta^k = 4(-1) = -4$.

So we have $\eta + \eta' = -1$ and $\eta \eta' = -4$.
Therefore, $\eta$ and $\eta'$ are the two roots of the quadratic polynomial $x^2 - (\eta + \eta')x + \eta \eta' = 0$ that is, $x^2 + x - 4 = 0$.
 Solving this quadratic equation gives $x = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt 17}{2}$.
Hence, $\eta = \frac{-1 + \sqrt 17}{2}$, $\eta' = \frac{-1 - \sqrt 17}{2}$.
It follows that both $\eta$ and $\eta'$ lie in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 17)$, and since $\eta \notin \mathbb{Q}$, we conclude $K_2 = \mathbb{Q}(\eta) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 17)$.

**Step 3: Construct $K_4$ (refined splitting)**
We now refine the quadratic split by decomposing the Gaussian periods further.
Recall from Step 2: $\eta = \sum_{k \in QR} \zeta^k = \frac{-1 + \sqrt 17}{2}$.

*Partition the quadratic residues*
The set of quadratic residues modulo $17$ has $8$ elements. We partition it into two subsets of $4$ elements each: $QR = C_0 \sqcup C_1$, chosen so that each subset is stable under a subgroup of index 2 in the Galois group.
Define: $\eta_0 = \sum_{k \in C_0} \zeta^k$, $\eta_1 = \sum_{k \in C_1} \zeta^k$.
Then: $\eta = \eta_0 + \eta_1$.

*Find a quadratic equation for $\eta_0$, $\eta_1$.*
Because the Galois group acts transitively on $C_0$ and $C_1$, the elements $\eta_0$ and $\eta_1$ are conjugate over $K_2 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 17)$.
Hence they satisfy a quadratic polynomial over $K_2$: $x^2 - (\eta_0 + \eta_1)x + \eta_0 \eta_1 = 0$.
We already know: $\eta_0 + \eta_1 = \eta$.
Using the same counting argument as in Step 2 (Gaussian period multiplication), one finds $\eta_0 \eta_1 = \frac{\eta-1}{4}$. 
Thus the quadratic equation becomes: $x^2 - \eta x + \frac{\eta - 1}{4} = 0$.

*Substitute the value of $\eta$*
Recall: $\eta = \frac{-1 + \sqrt 17}{2}$.
Substitute into the equation: $x^2 - \frac{-1+\sqrt 17}{2} x + \frac{\frac{-1+ \sqrt 17}{2} -1}{4} = 0$.
Simplify the constant term: $\frac{\frac{-1+\sqrt 17}{2} - 1}{4} = \frac{-3 + \sqrt 17}{8}$.
So the equation becomes: $x^2 - \frac{-1 + \sqrt 17}{2} x + \frac{-3+\sqrt 17}{8} = 0$.

*Solve using the quadratic formula*
The discriminant is: $\Delta = \eta^2 - 4 \cdot \frac{\eta - 1}{4} = \eta^2 - (\eta - 1)$.
Compute: $\eta^2 = (\frac{-1 + \sqrt 17}{2})^2 = \frac{18 - 2 \sqrt 17}{4}$.
Thus: $\Delta = \frac{18 - 2 \sqrt 17}{4} - \frac{-1 + \sqrt 17}{2} + 1$.
Simplifying gives: $\Delta = \frac{34 - 2 \sqrt 17}{4}$.
Hence: $\sqrt \Delta = \frac{\sqrt{34 - 2 \sqrt 17}}{2}$

*Write the roots explicitly*
Applying the quadratic formula: $\eta_0, \eta_1 = \frac{\eta \pm \sqrt \Delta}{2}$.
Substitute: $\eta_0, \eta_1 = \frac{ 1}{4}(-1 + \sqrt 17 \pm \sqrt {34 - 2 \sqrt 17})$.

Identify the field $K_4$.
Thus both $\eta_0$ and $\eta_1$ lie in: $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 17, \sqrt{34 - 2 \sqrt 17})$.
Since $\eta_0 \notin K_2$ adjoining $\sqrt{34 - 2 \sqrt 17}$ produces a quadratic extension of $K_2$.
Therefore: $K_4 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 17, \sqrt 34 - 2 \sqrt 17)$


**Step 4: Construct $K_8$ and extract cos $(2 \pi /17)$**
We now refine the decomposition once more to reach the maximal real subfield $K_8 = \mathbb{Q}(\eta + \eta^{-1})$.

*Final splitting*
From Step 3, write: $\eta_0 = \zeta_0 + \zeta_1$ where we choose: $\xi_0 = \zeta + \zeta^{-1}$, $\xi_1 = \zeta^4 + \zeta^{-4}$.
Thus: $\xi_0 + \xi_1 = \eta_0$

*Compute the product*
Multiply $\xi_0 \xi_1 = (\zeta + \zeta^{-1})(\zeta^4 + \zeta^{-4}) = \zeta^{5} + \zeta^{-5} + \zeta^3 + \zeta^{-3}$.
This is another Gaussian period, and from the known partition it equals: $\xi_0 \xi_1 = \eta_1$.

*Form the quadratic*
Hence $\xi_0$ and $\xi_1$ are roots satisfying: $x^2 - \eta_0 x + \eta_1 = 0$.

*Solve*
$\xi_0 = \frac{\eta_0 \pm \sqrt{\eta_0^2 -4 \eta_1}}{2}$.
Substituting the expression for $\eta_0$, $\eta_1$ from Step 3 and simplifying yields: 
$\xi_0 = \zeta + \zeta^{-1} = \frac{1}{8}(-1 + \sqrt 17 + \sqrt { 34 - 2 \sqrt 17 } + 2 \sqrt{17 + 3 \sqrt 17 - \sqrt{ 34 - 2 \sqrt 17} - 2 \sqrt{34 + 2 \sqrt 17}})$.

*Extract the cosine*
Using $\zeta + \zeta^{-1} = 2 cos ( 2 \pi / 17)$, we obtain $cos (2 \pi / 17) = - \frac{1}{16} + \frac{1}{16}\sqrt 17 + \frac{1}{16} \sqrt{34 - 2 \sqrt 17} + \frac{1}{8}\sqrt{17 + 3 \sqrt 17 - \sqrt{34 - 2 \sqrt 17} - 2 \sqrt {34 + 2 \sqrt 17}}$.

**Step 5: Verify explicit radical expression**
Compute:
- $\sqrt 17 \approx 4.1231$
- $\sqrt{ 34 - 2 \sqrt 17} \approx 5.074$
- $\sqrt{34 + 2 \sqrt 17} \approx \sqrt 42.246 \approx 6.500$ 
Now evaluate inner radical: $17 + 3\sqrt 17 - 5.074 - 2(6.500) = 17 + 12.369 - 5.074 -13.000 = 11.295$
$\sqrt 11.295 \approx 3.361$

Now sum: 
$-\frac{1}{16} \approx -0.0625$
$\frac{\sqrt 17}{16} \approx 0.2577$
$\frac{\sqrt{34 - 2 \sqrt 17}}{16} \approx 0.3171$
$\frac{1}{8}(3.361) \approx 0.4201$
Sum: $-0.0625 + 0.2577 + 0.3171 + 0.4201 \approx 0.9324$
$cos \frac{2 \pi}{17} \approx 0.93247$ 

Notes
- The resolvent method is purely algebraic but choosing the correct signs of radicals requires numerical evaluation, since multiple embeddings satisfy the same algebraic equations.
- Let $H \subset G = Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/17\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$. A subset $C \subset (\mathbb{Z}/17\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$ is stable under $H$ if $a \in C$, $h \in H \Rightarrow h \cdot a \in C$. Here the action is: $\sigma_b(\zeta^a) = \zeta^{ba}$, so the group acts on exponents by multiplication mod $17$.
- This problem solves a 2000-year-old geometric problem. The expression for $cos(\frac{2 \pi}{17})$ is equivalent to constructing a regular 17-gon with straightedge and compass. Since Greek antiquity, people knew how to construct polygons with $3, 4, 5, 6$ sides, etc. but $7,9,11,13,17$ were mysterious. In 1796, Gauss proved: the regular 17-gon is constructible. This was the first new constructible polygon discovered since ancient Greece. Your derivation explains why: $\mathbb{Q}(cos(2 \pi / 17))$ is built from a tower of quadratic extensions, so everything reduces to nested square roots, exactly what straightedge and compass allow.

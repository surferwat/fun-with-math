Show that a subgroup of index 2 is always normal.

Let $H$ be a subgroup of $G$ of index $2$. 
Then $H$ has exactly two presentations: $H$ itself and one other presentation consisting of all the elements of $G$ not in $H$.

Let $T \in G$. 
We must show that for every $S \in H$, $T^{-1}ST \in H$.

If $T \in H$, then $T^{-1} \in H$ since $H$ is a subgoup. Because $S,T^{-1},T \in H$ is closed under multiplication, it follows that $T^{-1}ST \in H$.

Now suppose $T \notin H$. 
Since there are only two presentations of $H$, multiplying every element of $H$ on the left by $T$ gives the other presentation, namely the complement $G \setminus H$. 
Likewise, multiplying every element of $H$ on the right by $T$ also gives the other presentation.
Thus, the two presentations obtained are the same: $TH = HT$.
Multiplying this equality on the left by $T^{-1}$ and on the right by $T^{-1}$ gives $H = T^{-1}HT$.
Hence, for every $S \in H$, $T^{-1}ST \in H$.

Since this holds for every $T \in G, H$ has the property singled out by Galois and is therefore a normal subgroup.

Notes
- Multiplying every element of $H$ on the left by $T$ means taking each element of $H$ and multiply it by $T$ on the left. If $H = \left\{ h_1, h_2, ..., h_n \right\}$, then $TH = \left\{ Th : h \in H \right\} = \left\{Th_1, Th_2, ..., Th_n \right\}$. This set $TH$ is what is called the left presentation (or in modern terminology, the left coset) determined by $T$. For example, let $G = S_3$, $H = \left\{ e, (123), (132)\right\}$, and let $T = (12)$. Then $TH = \left\{(12)e, (12)(123), (12)(132) \right\} = \left\{(12), (23), (13) \right\}$, which is exactly the other presentation of $H$.

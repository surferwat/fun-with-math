Fill out the following sketch of a solution of the quartic equation based on the resolvent $t = x + iy - z - ir$. (See Lagrange [L1], Section 48. Lagrange ascribes the method to Bezout.) This quantity satisfies an equation of degree 24 which is an equation of degree 6 in $t^4$. Let $\pi = t\tilde{t}$ where $\tilde{t}$ is the complex conjugate of $t$. Then $\pi$ is the root of a known cubic. Then $(i/2)(t+\tilde{t})(t-\tilde{t})$ is the difference of the other two roots of this cubic, and its square $-(t^4-2t^2t^2+\tilde{t}^4)/4$ can be expressed in terms of $\pi$. Thus $t$ satisfies a quadratic equation in $t^4$ whose coefficients are expressible in terms of $\pi$. A solution $x$ of the original equation can be expressed in terms of $t$, $\tilde{t}$, a known quantity, and $x-y+z-r$. The latter can be found as in $\S 17$ or in terms of other values of $t$.  

Let the quartic have roots $x,y,z,r$ and introduce the Lagrange resolvent $t=x+iy-z-ir$.

**Step 1: Equation satisfied by $t$.**

By permuting the roots $x,y,z,r$ and by replacing $i$ by $-i$, one obtains 24 distinct expressions of the same form as $t$. Hence $t$ satisfies an equation of degree 24 whose coefficients are symmetric functions of $x,y,z,r$. Since replacing $t$ by $it$ leaves the set of these expressions invariant, the equation may be written as an equation of degree $6$ in $t^4$.

Write $P(T) = a_{24} T^{24} + a_{23} T^{23} + ... + a_1 T + a_0, a_{24} = 1$.

*Comment: Replace $T$ by $iT$. Since multiplication of very root by $i$ merely permutes the set $\{t_1, ... , t_{24}\}$, the polynomial with roots $it_1, ..., it_{24}$ is the same polynomial. Thus $P(iT) = P(T)$. But $P(iT) = a_{24}i^{24}T^{24} + a_{23}i^{23}T^{23} + ... + a_1 i T + a_0$. Since $i^{24} = 1$ comparison of coefficients in $P(iT) = P(T)$ gives $a_k i^k = a_k$ for all $k$. Hence $a_k = 0$ unless $i^k = 1$ that is unless $k$ is divisible by $4$. Therefore the equation of degree $24$ satisfied by $t$ has the form $T^{24} + b_5 T^{20} + b_4 T^{16} + b_3 T^{12} + b_2 T^8 + b_1 T^4 + b_0 = 0$ and contains only powers of $T$ which are multiples of $4$. Equivalently, upon setting $U = T^4$, the equation becomes an equation of degree $6$ in $U$.

**Step 2: The product $\pi = t\tilde{t}$.**

Let $\tilde{t} = x - iy - z + ir$, and put $\pi = t \tilde{t}$. 
A direct computation gives $\pi = (x-z)^2 + (y-r)^2$. 
The three quantities obtained by pairing the four roots in all possible ways are $(x-z)^2+(y-r)^2$, $(x-y)^2+(z-r)^2$, $(x-r)^2+(y-z)^2$.
These three quantities satisfy a known cubic equation, the coefficients of which are symmetric functions of the roots and therefore expressible in terms of the coefficients of the quartic. Thus $\pi$ is determined by solving this cubic.

*Comment: Let $A = (x-z)^2 + (y-r)^2, B=(x-y)^2+(z-r)^2, C=(x-r)^2 +(y-z)^2$. Then $A,B,C$ are the roots of the cubic $(u-A)(u-B)(u-C)=u^3 - (A+B+C)u^2 + (AB + BC + CA)u - ABC$. The coefficients $A+B+C$, $AB+BC+CA$, and $ABC$ are symmetric polynomials in $x,y,z,r$ hence can be expressed in terms of the elementary symmetric functions $e_1 = x + y + z + r$, $e_2 = \sum xy$, $e_3 = \sum xyz$, $e_4 = xyzr$, which by Viete are the coefficients of the quartic. Therefore this cubic is explicitly known, and $\pi = (x-z)^2 + (y-r)^2$ is determined as one of its roots.*

**Step 3: Determination of t^4.**

The difference of the other two roots of this cubic is $i/2(t+\tilde{t})(t-\tilde{t})$.
Squaring, we obtain $-(t^2-\tilde{t}^2)^2/4 = -(t^4-2t^2\tilde{t}^2+\tilde{4}^4)/4$.
Since $t\tilde{t} = \pi$, the quantities $t^2\tilde{t}^2 = \pi^2$ and $t^4+\tilde{t}^4$ can be expressed in terms of $\pi$ (and the roots). Hence $t^4$ satisfies a quadratic equation whose coefficients are known once $\pi$ is known.

*Comment: $B-C = 2(x-z)(r-y) = i/2(t+\tilde{t})(t-\tilde{t})$. $t^4$ satisfies a quadratic because the product $t^4t^4$ determined by $\pi$ which is known and the sum $t^4$ + $t^4$ can be expressed in terms of $\pi$ and the quartic roots (or the resolvent cubic data like $B-C$) which are both known as soon as the cubic is solved. Thus, we can construct a quadratic with roots $t$ and $\tilde{t}$, whose coefficients are known once $\pi$ is known.*

**Step 4: Recovery of the roots.**

Fixing $\pi=A$ as a root of the resolvent cubic determines the other cubic roots $B$ and $C$. Then $t^4$ and $\tilde{t}^4$ satisfy a quadratic with known coefficients known in terms of $\pi$, whose solutions yields $t$ and $\tilde{t}$ from which the original quartic roots can be reconstructed.

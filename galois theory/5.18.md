Let $a,b,c, ...$ be the roots of an equation $f(x)=0$ and let $t$ be any primitive element of the field $K(a,b,c,...)$ (see Exercise 13 of the Fourth Set). Show that the irreducible equation with coefficients in $K$ of which $t$ is a root has degree equal to the number of elements in the Galois group of $f(x)=0$ over $K$, that it splits into linear factors over $K(t)$, and the Galois' presentation of the Galois group, $(1)$ of $\S41$, is valid for $t$ (even though $t$ is not necessarily a Galois resolvent).

Let $L=K(a,b,c,...) = K(t)$.
Let $G = \left\{S_1, S_2, ..., S_m \right\}$ be the Galois group of $f(x) =0$ over $K$, viewed as the group of automorphisms of $L$, and $m = \vert G \vert$.

Consider the polynomial $M(X) = \prod_{i=1}^m (X - S_i(t))$.
Since each automorphism of $G$ merely permutes the elements $S_i(t)$, the coefficients of $M(X)$ are invariant under every element of the Galois group. 
Hence they belong to $K$.

*Part 1: We show that the irreducible equation with coefficients in $K$ of which $t$ is a root has degree equal to the number of elements in the Galois group of $f(x)=0$ over $K$*
We claim that $M(X)$ is irreducible over $K$. 
Suppose $M(X)$ is reducible.
Let $H(X)$ be an irreducible factor of $M(X)$ in $K[X]$. 
Then $H(X)$ contains some root $S_i(t)$.
Since the coefficients of $H(X)$ lie in $K$, applying any $S_j \in G$ to $H(S_i(t))=0$ gives $H(S_jS_i(t))=0$.
As $S_jS_i$ ranges over all elements of $G$, $H(X)$ contains every root $S_k(t)$ of $M(X)$, so $H(X)=M(X)$, contradicting the assumption that $M(X)$ is reducible.
Thus, $M(X)$ is irreducible.

The roots $S_i(t)$ are distinct.
For if $S_i(t) = S_j(t)$, then $S_j^{-1} S_i(t) = t$.
Since $t$ is a primitive element, $L=K(t)$, so any automorphism fixing $t$ fixes every element of $L$. 
Hence $S_j^{-1}S_i$ is the identity, and therefore $S_i = S_j$.
Thus $M(X)$ has exactly $m$ distinct roots, so $\operatorname{deg} M = m = \vert G \vert = \vert \operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\vert$.

*Part 2: We show that it splits into linear factors over $K(t)$*
Moreover, every root of $M(X)$ is one of the elements $S_j(t)$, which all lie in $L = K(t)$. 
Hence $M(X)$ splits into linear factors over $K(t)$.

*Part 3: The Galois' presentation of the Galois group, $(1)$ of $\S41$, is valid for $t$ (even though $t$ is not necessarily a Galois resolvent)*
Finally, since $t$ is primitive, every root of $f(x)$ is a polynomial in $t$.
Thus, there exist polynomials $\phi_a(X), \phi_b(X), \phi_c(X), ... \in K[X]$ such that $a=\phi_a(t)$, $b=\phi_b(t)$, $c=\phi_c(t), ...$.
Applying the automorphisms $S_1, S_2, ..., S_m$ gives $S_i(a)=\phi_a(S_i(t)), S_i(b)=\phi_b(S_i(t)), S_i(c)=\phi_c(S_i(t)), ...$, for $i=1, ..., m$. 
These rows are precisely Galois' presentation of the Galois group.
Therefore, Galois' presentation remains valid for the primitive element $t$, even though $t$ is not necessarily a Galois resolvent.

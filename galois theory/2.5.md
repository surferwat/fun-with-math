Find the expression of $t_1 t_2 t_3=w$ in terms of $\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3, \sigma_4$ where as in $\S17$, $t_1= x-y+z-r$, $t_2 = x+y-z-r$, $t_3 = x-y-z+r$. 

Let $\sigma_1 = x + y + z + r$, $\sigma_2 = \sum xy$, $\sigma_3 = \sum xyz$, $\sigma_4 = xyzr$ be the elementary symmetric functions of $x,y,z,r$.

With $t_1=x-y+z-r$, $t_2=x+y-z-r$, $t_3 = x-y-z+r$, their product simplifies to a symmetric polynomial of degree $3$. After expansion and reduction in terms of the $\sigma_i$, one finds that the dependence on $\sigma_4$ cancels, and $w=t_1t_2t_3 = \sigma_1^3 - 4 \sigma_1 \sigma_2 + 8 \sigma_3$.

Notes:
- This problem shows how non-symmetric expressions become symmetric.
- Since is a symmetric function, the Fundamental Theorem of Symmetric Functions tells us that "Every symmetric polynomial can be expressed in terms of the elementary symmetric functions $\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3, \sigma_4$"
- Even though $x,y,z,r$ are four variables, the expression: has degree 3, is odd under certain sign changes, and involves only three linear factors. Hence, the highest-degree symmetric invariant $\sigma_4 = xyzr$ cannot occur. This teaches how degree and structure restrict which invariants appear.

Prove that if $K'$ is an extension of $K$ then the Galois group of $f(x)=0$ over $K'$ coincides with its Galois group over $K$ if and only if $K(a,b,c,\dots) \cap K'=K$. (Both $K(a,b,c,\dots)$ and $K'$ are contained in $K'(a,b,c,\dots)$.) In other words, the Galois group is reduced if and only if one of the newly adjoined quantities in $K'$ can be expressed rationally in terms of the roots of $f$ and previously known quantities. 

Let $L=K(a,b,c,\dots)$ be the field generated over $K$ by the roots of $f(x)=0$. 
Let $G_K$ be the Galois group over $K$, and let $G_{K'}$ be the Galois group over $K'$, viewed as a subgroup of $G_K$. 
We want to prove $G_{K'}=G_K$ if and only if $L\cap K'=K$.

First suppose that $L\cap K'\neq K$. 
Then there is an element $\beta\in L\cap K'$ with $\beta\notin K$. 
Since $\beta\notin K$, Proposition I tells us that $\beta$ is not invariant under all substitutions in $G_K$. 
Thus some substitution in $G_K$ moves $\beta$. 
However, $\beta\in K'$, so every substitution in $G_{K'}$ fixes $\beta$. 
Therefore $G_{K'}$ cannot equal $G_K$.

Conversely, suppose that $G_{K'}$ is a proper subgroup of $G_K$. 
Here we must be careful: we cannot simply choose an arbitrary $\beta\in L\setminus K$ and say that it is fixed by $G_{K'}$.
Instead, we use the result from §46, which says that whenever a subgroup is a proper subgroup of the full Galois group, there exists a special element $\beta\in L$ such that $\beta\notin K$ but every substitution in the smaller subgroup fixes $\beta$. 
Thus we obtain $\beta\in L\setminus K$ satisfying $\sigma(\beta)=\beta$ for every $\sigma\in G_{K'}$. 
At this point we do not yet know that $\beta\in K'$. 
However, since $G_{K'}$ is the Galois group over $K'$ and $\beta$ is invariant under every substitution in this group, Proposition I, applied with $K'$ in place of $K$, tells us that $\beta\in K'$. 
Therefore $\beta\in L\cap K'$ while $\beta\notin K$, so $L\cap K'\neq K$.

Thus $G_{K'}\neq G_K$ if and only if $L\cap K'\neq K$. 
Taking contrapositives gives $G_{K'}=G_K$ if and only if $L\cap K'=K$.

Author's solution
An element of $K'$ in $K(a,b,c,\dots)$ but not in $K$ would be moved by some element of the Galois group over $K$ (Proposition I) but by no element of the Galois group over $K'$, so the two groups would not coincide. Conversely, if the two groups do not coincide, the argument of $\S46$ shows that some element of $K(a,b,c,\dots)$ not in $K$ is invariant under the smaller group, i.e., is in $K'$.

Notes
- Proposition I. Let $\Psi(U, V, W, ...)$ be a polynomial in $n$ variables with coefficients in $K$. Let $\Psi_{t'}$ be the element of $K(a,b,c,...)$ obtained by setting $U = \phi_a(t'), V = \phi_b(t'), W = \phi_c(t'), ...$ in $\Psi$. In other words, for $U,V,W,...$ one substitutes the arrangement of the roots given in the row of the above table $(1)$ corresponding to $t'$. Similarly, let $\Psi_t, \Psi_{t''}, \Psi_{t'''}, ...$ be defined by substituting the corresponding row. Then $\Psi_t$ is in $K$ if and only if the elements $\Psi_t, \Psi_{t'}, \Psi_{t''}, ...$ are all equal. Loosely speaking, $\Psi(a,b,c,...)$ is a known quantity if and only if it is invariant under all the substitutions of the Galois group.
- $\S46$ Proposition. Let $G$ be the Galois group of $f(x)=0$ over $K$ and let $G'$ be a normal subgroup of $G$ of prime index $p$. Assume also that $K$ contains a primitive $p$th root of unity $\alpha$, that is, a solution of $\alpha^p=1$ with $\alpha \ne 1$. Then there is an element $k$ of $K$ such that if $K' \supset K$ is the field obtained by adjoining a $p$th root of $k$ of $K$ then the Galois group of $f(x)=0$ over $K'$ is $G'$.
- The central insight is: enlarging the base field $\leftrightarrow$ shrinking the Galois group and this problem gives a precise criterion for when that happens $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K') \ne \operatorname{Gal}(L/K) \iff L \cap K' \ne K$.

Galois changed his statement of Proposition II, possibly on the night before his duel, eliminating the assumption that the degree of the auxiliary equation be prime. In all likelihood he intended to state: If a root $r$ of an irreducible polynomial of degree $m$ is adjoined to $K$, then the index of the new Galois group (that of $f = 0$ over $K(r)$) in the old Galois group (that of $f = 0$ over $K$) is a divisor of $m$. Prove this. (A slight modification of the proof in the text). Historical note: Galois' actual statement that the old group is partitioned into "groups" shows that he was not using the word "group" in the modern sense, because it is not the substitutions, but the arrangements, that are partitioned. Each "group" in the partition is a presentation of a subgroup of the old group; these subgroups are conjugates (not necessarily distinct) of the new group. 

Let $f(X)\in K[X]$ be irreducible, let $L$ be its splitting field over $K$, and let $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$. 
Let $r$ be a root of an irreducible polynomial over $K$ of degree $m$, and put $K'=K(r)$. 
Let $G'$ denote the Galois group of $f=0$ over $K'$. 
Then, regarding $G'$ as a subgroup of $G$, $[G:G']\mid m$.

Galois' polynomial proof
Let $r=r^{(1)},r^{(2)},\ldots,r^{(m)}$ be the $m$ conjugates of $r$ over $K$. 
As in the proof of Proposition §44, let $G(X)$ be the polynomial corresponding to the old group and $H(X,r)$ the polynomial corresponding to the new group. 
For each conjugate $r^{(i)}$, the same argument as in §44 gives a factorization $G(X)=H(X,r^{(i)})Q(X,r^{(i)})$, where $Q(X,r^{(i)})$ is the remaining factor.
Multiplying these $m$ factorizations, we obtain $G(X)^m=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}H(X,r^{(i)})\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}Q(X,r^{(i)})\right)$.
Put $h(X)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}H(X,r^{(i)})$. 
We claim that $h(X)$ has coefficients in $K$. 
Indeed, any automorphism over $K$ merely permutes the conjugates $r^{(1)},\ldots,r^{(m)}$. 
It therefore merely permutes the factors $H(X,r^{(i)})$, leaving their product $h(X)$ unchanged. Hence every coefficient of $h(X)$ is fixed by these conjugations, and so $h(X)\in K[X]$.
The preceding factorization shows that $h(X)$ divides $G(X)^m$ in $K[X]$. 
Since $G(X)$ is irreducible over $K$, every divisor of $G(X)^m$ in $K[X]$ is, up to a nonzero constant factor, a power of $G(X)$. 
Thus $h(X)=cG(X)^j$ for some $c\in K^\times$ and some integer $j$, with $0\leq j\leq m$.
Taking degrees gives $m\deg H=j\deg G$. 
Therefore $m=j\frac{\deg G}{\deg H}$. The quotient $\frac{\deg G}{\deg H}$ is precisely the index $[G']$ of the new group in the old group. 
Hence $m=j[G']$, and therefore $[G']\mid m$.
Thus $[G']\mid m$.

Field-theoretic proof
Let $E=L\cap K'$. 
Since $K'/K$ has degree $m$, we have $[E:K]\mid[K':K]=m$. 
We claim that $G'=\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)$. 
Indeed, an element $\sigma\in G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ belongs to the Galois group over $K'$ precisely when it fixes every element of $K'$ that lies in $L$. 
But the elements of $K'$ lying in $L$ are exactly the elements of $E=L\cap K'$. 
Thus $G'=\left\{ \sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/K):\sigma|_E=\operatorname{id} \right\}=\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)$. 
Consequently, by the fundamental theorem of Galois theory, $[G:G']=[E:K]$. 
Combining this with $[E:K]\mid m$ gives $[G:G']\mid m$.

Author's solution:
As in the proof of $\S44$, $G(X)=H(X,r^{(i)})Q(X,r^{(i)})$ for all $m$ roots $r,r',r'',\dots,r^{(m-1)}$ of the auxiliary equation. Then $G(X)^m = h(X)q(X)$ where $h(X)=H(X,r)H(X,r')H(X,r'') \dots$. This gives $h(X) =$ const. $G(X)^j$ for some $j$, from which $m=j \cdot (\deg G/\deg H)$ as desired.

Notes
- $K'/K$ is read as "$K$ prime over $K$" and means the field extension of $K$obtained by adjoining whatever is needed to get $K'$.
- $G'={\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/K):\sigma|_E=\operatorname{id}}=\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)$. We have three fields: $K \subseteq E \subseteq L$ where $E=L \cap K'$. The old Galois group $G=Gal(L/K)$. This means: $G$ consists of all automorphisms of $L$ that every element of $K$ fixed. Now, what is $G'$? When we replace $K$ by $K'$, we want the automorphisms to fix $K'$ as well. But there is an important issue: the automorphisms in $Gal(L/K)$ only act on elements of $L$. So we only care about the part of $K'$ that is actually inside $L$. That part is $E$. Therefore, $G'$ consists exactly of the automorphisms in $G$ that fix every element of $E$. In symbols: $G' = \left\{ \sigma \in Gal(L/K) : \sigma \text{ fixes every element of E } \right\}$. Now consider the notation $\sigma |_{e} = id$. This simply means: "$\sigma$ restricted to E is the identity." In plain English: $\sigma$ does nothing to any element of $E$. So $\sigma |_{e} = id$ means $\sigma(a) = a \text{ for every } a \in E$. Therefore, $\left\{ \sigma \in Gal(L/K) : \sigma |_{e} = id \right\}$ just means: "All automorphisms of $L$ that fix $K$ and also fix $E$." But since $E$ contains $K$, any automorphism that fixes $E$ automatically fixes $K$. Thus saying "fix $E$" is enough. And by definition, the group of automorphisms of $L$ that fix $E$ is $Gal(L/E)$. Therefore, $G' = Gal(L/E)$.  
- The field-theoretic proof makes transparent why the conclusion is only divisibility rather than equality: only the part of $K(r)$ contained in the splitting field $L$ affects the Galois group. In fact, $[G:G']=[L\cap K(r):K]\mid[K(r):K]=m$.

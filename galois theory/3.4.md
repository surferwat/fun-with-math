Show that if $j$ and $k$ are relatively prime then the product of a primitive $j$th root of unity and a primitive $k$th root of unity is a primitive $jk$th root of unity.

Let $\zeta_j$ be a primitive $j$th root of unity, so $\zeta_j^j = 1$ and no smaller positive power equals $1$.
Let $\zeta_k$ be a primitive $k$th root of unity, so $\zeta_k^k=1$ and its order is $k$.
Assume $gcd(j,k)=1$.
We prove that $\zeta_j \zeta_k$ is a primitive $jk$th root of unity.

**Step 1: Show it is a $jk$th root of unity**
$(\zeta_j \zeta_k)^{jk} = \zeta_j^{jk} \zeta_k^{jk}$.
Since $\zeta_j^j = 1$, $\zeta_j^{jk} = (\zeta_j^j)^k = 1^k = 1$.
Similarly, since $\zeta_k^k = 1$, $\zeta_j^{jk} = (\zeta_k^k)^j = 1^j = 1$.
Therefore, $(\zeta_j \zeta_k)^{jk} = 1$.
So, $\zeta_j \zeta_k$ is a $jk$th root of unity.

**Step 2: Show its order is exactly $jk$**
Suppose $(\zeta_j \zeta_k)^n = 1$.
Then $\zeta_j^n \zeta_k^n = 1$
So $\zeta_j^n = \zeta_k^{-n}$
Now, the left side is a $j$th root of unity and the right side is a $k$th root of unity. 
Thus, $\zeta_j^n$ is a simultaneously a $j$th and $k$th root of unity.
Hence its order divides both $j$ and $k$.
Since $gcd(j,k)=1$, the only possibility is that its order is $1$.
Therefore, since $ord(\zeta_j) = j$ and $\zeta_j^n=1$, by the general fact $x^m= 1 \Rightarrow ord(x) | m$, $j | n$.
Similarly, since $ord(\zeta_k) = k$ and $\zeta_k^n = 1$,  $k | n$.
Since $j$ and $k$ are relatively prime $jk | n$.
Thus the smallest positive integer $n$ for which $(\zeta_j \zeta_k)^n = 1$ is $n = jk$.

**Conclusion**
The order of $\zeta_j \zeta_k$ is exactly $jk$.
Therefore, if $j$ and $k$ are relatively prime, the product of a primitive $j$th root of unity and a primitive $k$th root of unity is a primitive $jk$th root of unity.

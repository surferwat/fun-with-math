In the case $p=19$ of the construction of $\S25$, find a sequence of two intermediate fields $K_1 \subset K_d \subset K_{d'} \subset K_{18}$ where the inclusions are all strict inclusions. Show that  for any $p$ the maximum number of such intermediate fields is equal to one less than the number of prime factors of $p-1$, counted with multiplicities.

In $\S25$ the field constructed is $K_{18} = Q(\zeta)$ where $\zeta$ is a primitive 19th root of unity. It is known that the degree of $K_{18}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is  $[K_{18} : \mathbb{Q}] = \phi(19) = 18$. 
Earlier in the construction Harold defines fields $K_d$ for each divisor $d | (p-1)$ with $[K_d : \mathbb{Q}] = d$ and $K_d \subset K_{d'}$ whenever $d | d'$.
Thus the intermediate fields correspond to divisors of $p-1$.

**Step 1: Factor $p-1$**
$19-1 = 18 = 2 \cdot 3^2$
The divisors of $18$ include $1,2,3,6,9,18$.
To obtain a chain of strict inclusions we choose divisors increasing by divisibility. For example $1 | 2 | 6 | 18$.
This gives the fields $K_1 \subset K_2 \subset K_6 \subset K_{18}$.
Since $[K_1 : \mathbb{Q}] = 1$, $[K_2 : \mathbb{Q}] = 2$, $[K_6 : \mathbb{Q}] = 6$, $[K_{18} : \mathbb{Q}] = 18$, each inclusion is strict. Thus a suitable choice is $K_1 = \mathbb{Q}$, $K_d = K_2$, $K_{d'} = K_6$.
Hence $\mathbb{Q} \subset K_2 \subset K_6 \subset K_{18}$.

**Step 2: Maximum possible length of such a chain**
Let $p-1 = q_1^{e_1}q_2^{e_2}...q_r^{e_r}$ be the prime factorization of $p-1$.
Every intermediate field $K_d$ corresponds to a divisor $d | (p-1)$, and $[K_d : \mathbb{Q}] = d$.
Also $K_d \subset K_{d'} \iff d | d'$.
Thus a chain of intermediate fields $K_1 \subset K_{d_1} \subset K_{d_2} \subset ... \subset K_{p-1}$ corresponds exactly to a chain of divisors $1 < d_1 < d_2 < ... < p-1$ s.t. each divides the next.

**Step 3: Constructing the longest possible chain**
Write $p-1 = q_1^{e_1}q_2^{e_2}...q_r^{e_r}$.
Starting from $1$, we can build divisors by introducing the prime factors one at a time: $1, q_1, q_1^2, ..., q_1^{e_1}, q_1^{e_1}q_2,q_1^{e_1}q_2^2, ...$ and continuing until $q_1^{e_1}q_2^{e_2}...q_r^{e_r} = p -1$.
Each step multiplies by exactly one prime factor.
The total number of such steps equals $e_1 + e_2 + ... + e_r$. 
Thus the chain contains $e_1 + e_2 + ... + e_r + 1$ fields including the endpoints $K_1$ and $K_{p-1}$.
Therefore the number of intermediate fields between them is $(e_1 + e_2 + ... + e_r) - 1$.

**Step 4: Check for $p=19$**
$18 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 3$
Number of prime factors (with multiplicity): $3$.
Maximum intermediate fields: $3-1=2$.
Our chain $\mathbb{Q} \subset K_2 \subset K_6 \subset K_{18}$ indeed has exactly two intermediate fields.

**Conclusion**
For $p=19$ a suitable chain is $\mathbb{Q} \subset K_2 \subset K_6 \subset K_{18}$.
In general, if $p-1 = q_1^{e_1}q_2^{e_2}...q_r^{e_r}$ then the maximum number of intermediate fields in such a chain equals $(e_1 + e_2 + ... + e_r) - 1$, which is one less than the number of prime factors of $p-1$, counted with multiplicities.

Notes
- The $p-th$ cyclotomic field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)$ has degree $p-1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. The intermediate fields $K_d$ correspond to divisors of $p-1$, showing a clear, concrete hierarchy of subfields. This gives a first glimpse of how a field can contain strictly smaller fields, each with its own dimension over $\mathbb{Q}$, without yet using Galois theory.
- The maximum number of intermediate fields depends entirely on the prime factorization of $p-1$. This shows a deep link between number theory (factorization) and field theory (subfield structure). For example, for $p=19, 18 = 2 \cdot 3^2$ gives exactly $2$ intermediate fields in the longest chain. It's a concrete example of how the arithmetic of $p-1$ controls the shape of the field extensions.

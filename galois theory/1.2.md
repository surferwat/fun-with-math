Show that the formula $x,y = \frac{s\pm \sqrt{s^2-4p}}{2}$ of $\S{3}$ solves the problem algebraically in the sense that it gives a solution whenever $\sqrt{s^2-4p}$ is something with the property that its square is $s^2 - 4p$. Thus, in particular, it is valid for $s^2 - 4p < 0$ when $\sqrt{s^2-4p}$ is regarded as a complex number. 

Let $x =  \frac{s+ \sqrt{s^2-4p}}{2}$ and $y = \frac{s- \sqrt{s^2-4p}}{2}$.
Let $s=x+y$ and $p=xy$.
For $s=x+y$, substituting $x$ and $y$, we have:
$s = \frac{s+ \sqrt{s^2-4p}}{2} + \frac{s - \sqrt{s^2-4p}}{2} = \frac{2s}{2} = s$.
For $p = xy$, substituting $x$ and $y$, we have:
$p = \frac{s+ \sqrt{s^2-4p}}{2} \cdot \frac{s- \sqrt{s^2-4p}}{2} = \frac{s^2+s\sqrt{s^2-4p}-s \sqrt{s^2-4p}-(\sqrt{s^2-4p})^2}{4}=\frac{s^2+s\sqrt{s^2-4p}-s \sqrt{s^2-4p}-(s^2-4p)}{4}=p$.

Notes
- I believe the Edwards states that "the formula gives a solution whenever $\sqrt{s^2−4p}$ is something with the property that its square is $s^2−4p$" since the expression, $\frac{s^2+s\sqrt{s^2-4p}-s \sqrt{s^2-4p}-(s^2-4p)}{4}$, reduces to $p$  iff $(\sqrt{s^2−4p})^2=s^2−4p$.

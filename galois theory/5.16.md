Let $K$ be a field which contains a $p$th root of unity $\alpha \ne 1$ where $p$ is prime. Show that, as stated in $\S42$, if $a$ and $b$ are any two roots of $x^p - k = 0 (k \in K)$ then $b = \alpha^ja$ for some integer $j$.

Let $a$ and $b$ be roots of the polynomial $x^p - k$, $k \in K$.
Then $a^p =k, b^p = k$.

If $k=0$, then $a^p = b^p =0$.
Since a field has no nonzero nilpotent elements, it follows that $a=b=0$.
Hence $b=\alpha^0a$ so the result holds.

Now suppose $k \ne 0$. 
Then $a \ne 0$ and $b \ne 0$, so we may form the quotient $\frac{b}{a}$.
Since $a^p = b^p = k$, $(\frac{b}{a})^p = \frac{b^p}{a^p} = \frac{k}{k}=1$.
Thus $b/a$ is a $p$th root of unity.
Since $\alpha$ is a primitive $p$th root of unity, the $p$th roots of unity are precisely $1,\alpha, \alpha^2, ..., \alpha^{p-1}$.
Therefore, since $(\frac{b}{a})^p = 1$, it follows that $\frac{b}{a} = \alpha^j$ for some integer $j$.
Multiplying both sides by $a$ gives $b=\alpha^j a$.

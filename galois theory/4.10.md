In the case of the equation $x^3-r=0$ express $c$ as a polynomial in $t$. Since the ratio of any two roots is a cube root of unity, the field $K(t)$ must contain a square root of $-3$. Find one. 

Let $t$ be a root of $x^3-r=0$, so that $t^3=r$. 
The other two roots are $\omega t$, $\omega^2 t$, where $\omega$ is a primitive cube root of unity: $\omega^3=1$, $1+\omega+\omega^2 = 0$. 
For a cubic with roots $t_1$, $t_2$, $t_3$, define $c = (t_1-t_2)(t_1-t_3)(t_2-t_3)$.
Substituting the three roots gives $c= (t-\omega t)(t-\omega^2t)(\omega t - \omega^2t) =t^3(1-\omega)(1-\omega^2)(\omega-\omega^2)$.
Multiplying out, we have $(1-\omega)(1-\omega^2)=1-(\omega+ \omega^2)+\omega^3$.
Using $\omega + \omega^2 = -1$, $\omega^3 = 1$, we obtain $(1-\omega)(1-\omega^2)=3$.
Therefore $c=3t^3(\omega-\omega^2)$.
Finally, $(\omega - \omega^2)^2 = \omega^2 - 2\omega^3 + \omega^4 = \omega^2 + \omega-2$.
Because $\omega + \omega^2 = -1$, this becomes $(\omega - \omega^2)^2 = -3$.
Hence $\omega - \omega^2$ is a square root of $-3$.
Also, $\omega = \frac{\omega t}{t}$, the ratio of two roots, so $\omega \in K(t)$. Therefore $\omega - \omega^2 \in K(t)$, and thus $K(t)$ contains a square root of $-3$.

Notes
- This problem shows that the splitting field of $x^3 - r$ automatically contains $\sqrt{-3}$ revealing that solving a polynomial forces adjoining additional "symmetry" elements (cube roots of unity), not just the roots themselves. In other words, even if you only adjoin one root, the algebraic relationships among all roots force additional numbers (like roots of unity) to appear in the same field.
- Once you arrive at $c=3t^3(\omega-\omega^2)$, the expression $\omega-\omega^2$ stands out as the "mysterious extra factor." Since $3t^3 \in K(t)$, any square root of $-3$ has to come from $\omega$-part. So the natural thing is: check what happens when you square it. This is a common move in algebra: you isolate the unfamiliar factor, then compute a low power of it (usually square or cube), hoping it collapses using the defining relations. Here the defining relations are $\omega^3=1$, $1+\omega + \omega^2=0$, and indeed the square simplifies dramatically.
